# R05 — The consolidation run: Phases I, II and III brought under the chapter limit, and the record's redundant operations merged

*A revision without a provision, run under DIRCP02-1, DIRCP03-1 and DIRCP04-2 on the user's decision recorded at CP04. It merges events and restatements; it changes no outcome, moves no end vector and leaves the end state where it was.*

## Why

The chapters had grown past the point where a memorandum could read them. CP04 recorded Phase I at about 31,000 words and Phase II at about 33,700, both twice the 15,000-word limit, and Phase III over it; Phase VI was listed beside them (CP04 Part 1 §2). Most of the growth was of two kinds. Each memorandum had added its own institution as its own event on 1 January 1964, so that Phase I opened with eight events in which a decree was signed and an office stood up. And each memorandum that touched a battle had added a paragraph restating the battle's losses with its own increment, so that Dong Xoai carried five successive figures for the allied dead and the Ia Drang four. The user also asked this run to find the operations and use cases that were redundant, stale or could be combined, and to act on them.

The method is the one DIRCP02-1 and DIRCP03-1 lay down. Events that told the same thing are merged into one summary event, `EVR05-k`, and each merged event is kept as a `CANCELLED` stub pointing to the event that now carries it, so every citation of an old ID in the memoranda, the positions and the studies still leads somewhere. Where one event clearly carried the others, it absorbs them under its own ID instead. Successive restatements of one figure are replaced by the last figure, given once with its parts. The overviews and endings are rewritten by subject rather than by memorandum. Every figure a position, a directive or a later memorandum relies on is kept.

## The Ghostriders at Junction City

CP04 found that EVCP01-4 did not mention the gunships over Junction City's night attacks. The 4th Squadron was over every allied position attacked at night in III Corps from 1964, and the event now says that at Suoi Tre and Ap Gu the Ghostriders lit the treelines and fired on the assault waves forming beyond the wire, and at Prek Klok found the mortars [X-R05]. I do not adjust the historical returns. The fights were decided at the wire, and the enemy's losses were never counted closely enough for the gunships to show in them, which is the ground on which CP01 left the rifles out of the same returns (POS004-1). The Long Tan fight in the same event was the Australians' and the Nui Dat guns', and the record places no gunship there.

**Stale.** EC005-8, "the Haiphong plan in the drawer (May 1972)", and EC005-9, "the fixed-target strike in the North", were written while the North was barred by the historical restrictions. Memorandum 013's suspension of that constraint, and HAMMER FALL on 2 March 1965, put the plan into effect seven years earlier (EV013-1), so neither concept describes anything that can now happen. They stand in memorandum 005's record as written, and are not to be cited as live.

The siege concepts, EC005-3 the siege box and EC015-4 the night shift over the siege, work together rather than duplicate each other, and stay separate.

## What this run does not change

No event's outcome changes. No end vector moves; the end vectors' notes that cite merged IDs still resolve through the stubs. No settled position is reversed, and the end state in `history/endstate.toml` is unchanged. Phases IV, V and VI are not staged. The one new judgment is the gunships at Junction City, and it moves no figure.

On the user's two rulings about writing, the rewritten events try to tell a scene where they tell anything: Long Huu's corporal keeping a third of his ammunition, the 272nd's commander watching the flanking companies come through the rubber, Sergeant Muoi's third line in the district log. Where a passage only stated facts, it was cut or put into a table, as the tables above are. A rule saying so belongs in the standing directives, and it is entered below.
