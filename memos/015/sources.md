# Sources — Memorandum 015

One line per source, numbered in order of first use. Cite as [S1], [S2] … in the text.

[S1] AC-130J Ghostrider fact sheet — Air Force Special Operations Command, https://www.afsoc.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/2547234/ac-130j-ghostrider/ — crew of two pilots, two combat systems officers and four special mission aviators; four Rolls-Royce AE 2100D3 at 4,700 shp; 3,000 miles range limited by crew duty day, extendable by air refuelling; 30mm and 105mm trainable cannons; GBU-39, GBU-69, AGM-114, AGM-176; two EO/IR sensors; 164,000 lb maximum take-off weight; wingspan 132 ft 7 in, length 97 ft 9 in, height 39 ft 2 in; $165 million unit cost; 37 aircraft by FY24; IOC 2017; missions close air support, air interdiction and armed reconnaissance
[S2] AC-130J Ghostrider — Airforce Technology, https://www.airforce-technology.com/projects/ac-130j-ghostrider-aircraft/ — GAU-23 30mm chain gun; 362 kt at 22,000 ft; 28,000 ft ceiling with a 42,000 lb payload; an all-weather synthetic aperture radar pod (trade report, not confirmed in the operational test reports); AN/ALR-56M, AN/AAR-47(V)2, AN/ALE-47, provision for LAIRCM; lightweight composite armour and fuel-tank ullage explosion protection; helmet-mounted cueing; IOC September 2017; first Block 30 delivered March 2019, first combat mission June 2019
[S3] AC-130U fact sheet — US Air Force, https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104486/ac-130u/ — crew of 13 (five officers, eight enlisted, four of them aerial gunners); four Allison T56-A-15 at 4,300 shp; 300 mph at sea level; about 1,300 nm range, extendable by air refuelling; 25,000 ft ceiling; 155,000 lb maximum take-off weight; 25mm, 40mm and 105mm trainable guns; multispectral TV, high-definition infrared and synthetic aperture strike radar; $210 million; deployed 1995; radar first used to find and attack targets in Kosovo, 1999; 17 in service before retirement, last retired 26 June 2020; Vietnam gunships destroyed more than 10,000 trucks
[S4] FY2020 Annual Report, AC-130J Ghostrider — Director, Operational Test and Evaluation, https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2020/af/2020ac130j.pdf — Block 30: MX-20 and MX-25 EO/IR and laser-designator turrets; dual special mission processors; up to eight GBU-39/B, laser SDB or AGM-114 on wing racks; ten launch tubes in the cargo door for AGM-176 and GBU-69/B; nine aircrew (two pilots, CSO, WSO, five special mission aviators); gun and sensor malfunctions throughout the evaluation; usability "marginal"; RFCM and infrared suppression still future upgrades; no radar listed
[S5] AC-130U Spooky — GlobalSecurity.org, https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/systems/aircraft/ac-130u.htm — GAU-12/U at 1,800 rounds a minute replacing the H's two M61A1; 40mm Bofors L/60; modified 105mm M102; AN/APQ-180 radar for night and adverse weather; all-light-level television and FLIR; dual-target fire control, two targets up to 1 km apart on two sensors and two guns; more than 609,000 lines of code; INS and GPS; loiter up to 4.5 hours; orbit 5,000–10,000 ft above ground in a 5 nm arc; armour protection system; programme of 13, then 17 airframes
[S6] "Gunships" — Air Force Magazine (Air & Space Forces), June 2015, https://www.airandspaceforces.com/PDF/MagazineArchive/Magazine%20Documents/2015/June%202015/0615gunships.pdf — 135 aircraft converted (53 AC-47, 30 AC-130, 26 AC-119G, 26 AC-119K); peak of 53 gunships deployed, about half on the Trail; AC-47 combat from December 1964 in the Delta, 1,500 ft attack altitude; AC-47s in Thailand from early 1966, two interdiction sorties a night, back to the South in August 1966; last USAF AC-47 mission December 1969; AC-119G combat January 1969, outpost defence; AC-119K combat November 1969, 2.14 trucks a sortie in Commando Hunt VII; AC-130 combat test September 1967, about three times the AC-47 against trucks; AC-130E 2×20mm, 40mm, 105mm; AC-130s 5.37 trucks a sortie and almost 70% of gunship truck kills in Commando Hunt VII; F-4 0.29 a sortie; claims of more than 35,000 trucks of which CIA and DIA discounted 75%; North Vietnam imported almost 10,000 trucks a year; by 1972 gunships could not survive on the Trail; Commando Hunt VIII and IX cancelled
[S7] "All Vietnam War Gunship/Gunshipper Losses" — AC-119 Gunship Association, https://www.ac119gunships.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/All-Vietnam-War-Gunship-Gunshipper-Losses.pdf — 19 AC-47D, 5 AC-119 (2 G, 3 K) and 6 AC-130 (5 A, 1 H) lost, with dates and places; about 158–163 gunship crewmen killed
[S8] Gunship III: Shadows and Stingers — National Museum of the US Air Force, https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Visit/Museum-Exhibits/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/195862/gunship-iii-shadows-and-stingers/ — AC-119G replaced the AC-47, combat January 1969 with the 71st SOS, four 7.62mm miniguns; AC-119K first combat November 1969, four miniguns and two 20mm; both types transferred to the VNAF as the war ended
[S9] "Vietnamization" — Air & Space Forces Magazine, https://www.airandspaceforces.com/article/vietnamization/ — AC-47 and AC-119 gunships added to the VNAF under Vietnamization; VNAF flew 63% of South Vietnam's combat sorties in 1971; technical training in English limited to proficient students, about 6,000 pages of helicopter manuals untranslated in May 1971; the SA-7 made VNAF pilots reluctant to fly below 10,000 ft; in 1975 radar guns and SA-7s kept VNAF aircraft out of most battle areas
