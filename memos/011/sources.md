# Sources — Memorandum 011

[S1] "Iraq Versus Vietnam: A Comparison of Public Opinion" — Gallup, https://news.gallup.com/poll/18097/iraq-versus-vietnam-comparison-public-opinion.aspx — the Vietnam "mistake" question first asked August 1965; about six months into the large-scale commitment 24% mistake, 60% not a mistake; July 1967 41% / 48%; October 1967 47% / 44%; August 1968 53% mistake; 1969–70 average 55%; 1971 60%; 1973 60%.
[S2] "Induction Statistics" — Selective Service System, https://sss.gov/history-and-records/induction-statistics — inductions by year: 1964 112,386; 1965 230,991; 1966 382,010; 1967 228,263; 1968 296,406; 1969 283,586; 1970 162,746; 1971 94,092; 1972 49,514; 1973 646; Vietnam War total 1,857,304 for August 1964 – February 1973.
[S3] "Names on the Wall: A Closer Look at Those Who Died in Vietnam" — HistoryNet, https://www.historynet.com/names-on-the-wall-a-closer-look-at-those-who-died-in-vietnam/ — of enlisted dead, 63.3% volunteers; of all dead almost 70% volunteers; almost half of Army enlisted dead volunteers; all Marine Corps, Navy and Air Force enlisted dead volunteers.
[S4] "Watergate Babies" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watergate_Babies — the 1974 midterm elections: Democrats gained 49 House seats and 5 Senate seats.
[S5] "Ending the Vietnam War, 1969–1973" — Office of the Historian, US Department of State, https://history.state.gov/milestones/1969-1976/ending-vietnam — Nixon's letter to Thieu of 14 November 1972 that the United States "will react very strongly and rapidly to any violation of the agreement"; similar assurances by letters and envoys, understood on both sides to mean the return of the B-52s; Watergate weakened and distracted Nixon and, with congressional reluctance and economic constraints, the commitments were not upheld.
[S6] "Case–Church Amendment" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case%E2%80%93Church_Amendment — passed the House 325–86 on 26 June 1973 and the Senate 73–16 on 29 June 1973; conference report agreed 30 June; signed 1 July 1973; barred appropriated funds for combat by US forces in or over Cambodia, Laos and North and South Vietnam or off their shores, with all funds barred after 15 August 1973; Republicans relented on support for South Vietnam under the pressure of Watergate.
[S7] "Draft evasion in the Vietnam War" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draft_evasion_in_the_Vietnam_War — about 210,000 men formally accused of draft offences and about 570,000 classified as draft offenders; 8,750 convicted and 3,250 jailed; 30,000 to 100,000 left the country, mostly for Canada; more than half of 27 million eligible men deferred, exempted or disqualified.
[S8] "Public Opinion and the Calley Verdict" (Gallup for Newsweek, April 1971) — Digital History, University of Houston, https://www.digitalhistory.uh.edu/active_learning/explorations/vietnam/survey_q3.cfm — 522 adults: 11% approved the verdict, 15% disapproved saying it was no crime, 56% disapproved saying others shared responsibility; 70% said Calley was a scapegoat; 79% said the life sentence too harsh.
[S9] Foreign Relations of the United States, 1969–1976, Volume X, Vietnam, January 1973–July 1975, Document 217, Editorial Note — Office of the Historian, https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v10/d217 — President Ford's address to a joint session of Congress on 10 April 1975 asking $722 million in military assistance and $250 million in economic and humanitarian aid for South Vietnam by 19 April.
[S10] "Timeline of the Watergate scandal" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watergate_timeline — the first break-in at the Democratic National Committee offices 28 May 1972; the burglars arrested at 2:30 a.m. on 17 June 1972; Nixon re-elected 7 November 1972; the Senate Watergate Committee's televised hearings from 17 May 1973; the Saturday Night Massacre 20 October 1973; House Judiciary Committee articles of impeachment 27–30 July 1974; resignation 9 August 1974; Ford's pardon 8 September 1974.
[S11] "Nixon's Image Remains Negative Years After Watergate" — Gallup, https://news.gallup.com/poll/3670/nixons-image-remains-negative-years-after-watergate.aspx — Nixon's job approval 24% in early August 1974; 57% before the resignation said his conduct warranted removal from office.
[S12] "Volunteers" (design assignment, citing the Senate Armed Services Committee, Report on Selective Service and Military Compensation, February 1971, and VFW and Department of Defense statistics) — George Mason University, https://mason.gmu.edu/~kbateman/hist697sp03/design_assign/design_assign.htm — 9.2 million in service August 1964 – May 1975; 3.4 million in the Southeast Asia theater, 2.5 million in South Vietnam; 648,500 draftees served in South Vietnam January 1965 – May 1973; nearly two-thirds of those in the theater volunteers.
