# Sources — Memorandum 006

One line per source, numbered in order of first use. Cite as [S1], [S2] … in the text.

[S1] "Phoenix Program" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_program — ICEX formed June–July 1967 from Robert Komer's and Nelson Brickham's design; Phung Hoang decree December 1967; Thieu's decree July 1968; 81,740 VCI "neutralized" 1968–72, of whom 26,369 killed; about 87% of those killed died in conventional military operations, not Phoenix operations; quotas produced false arrests, bribes for release and torture; conviction required three independent sources, sentences up to six years; CIA Counter-Terror teams, later Provincial Reconnaissance Units, about 140 teams of 3–12 men by April 1965; programme officially ended December 1972; Colby's and communist accounts that 1968–72 was the infrastructure's hardest period
[S2] "F3EA — a targeting paradigm for contemporary warfare" — Australian Army Journal vol. 10 no. 1, Australian Army Research Centre, https://researchcentre.army.gov.au/library/australian-army-journal-aaj/volume-10-number-1-autumn/f3ea-targeting-paradigm-contemporary-warfare — the find, fix, finish, exploit, analyse cycle as the de facto standard for targeting insurgent networks in Iraq and Afghanistan; "exploit-analyse is the main effort" (Flynn); fast loops from tactical questioning and slow loops of network analysis; heavy consumption of ISR and analysts concentrated in one targeting agency; risk of chasing the lowest-level targets; poor fit for conventional campaigns
[S3] "Marines, Soldiers Get Biometrics Tool That ID'd Bin Laden" — Military.com Kit Up, August 2013, https://www.military.com/kitup/2013/08/marines-soldiers-biometrics-tool.html — SEEK II records fingerprints, iris and face and matches them against an onboard watchlist of up to 120,000 people; iris matching at 500,000 samples a second; 334 kits to the Marines in a year; at least 15,000 Cross Match units shipped worldwide; Marines enrolled 19,000 Afghans, of whom 300 were watchlisted
[S4] "Vietnam War: The Individual Rotation Policy" — HistoryNet, https://historynet.com/vietnam-war-the-individual-rotation-policy/ — the 12-month tour tied to the two-year draft term; Secretary of the Army Resor's proposal of a 15-month tour (about 20% fewer replacements); Westmoreland's six-month command tours from 1965; 130 senior officers given 19-month tours in 1966; no reserve mobilization; a veteran's account of five platoon leaders and four company commanders in one year; combat effectiveness estimated to peak and decline after about 180 days
[S5] "U.S. Army Unit Manning" — Center for Strategic and Budgetary Assessments, 2004 — Vietnam DEROS of 12 months (Army) and 13 months (Marines); Korea replacements in packets of four from 1952; COHORT under General Meyer, 1979–83, companies of about 100–180 kept together for three years; leadership failures and the finding that stability alone did not make effective units; a three-year unit life cycle; 35–40% of a division nondeployable in the Balkans rotations
[S6] "Army to shorten deployments to 9 months" — U.S. Army, https://www.army.mil/article/63073 — 9-month deployments for brigade combat teams and other units from 1 January 2012; 12-month tours before, with a goal of 24 months at home between deployments
[S7] "Army Unit Cohesion in Vietnam: A Bum Rap" — Defense Technical Information Center, https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA515790.pdf — argues that individual rotation did not by itself destroy cohesion in Vietnam; no conclusive studies link the replacement system to the late-war breakdown; cohesion followed leadership and the war's political course
[S8] "50 years ago, Army executes rapid buildup in Vietnam" — U.S. Army, https://www.army.mil/article-amp/153312/50_years_ago_army_executes_rapid_buildup_in_vietnam — about 50,000 US troops in early 1965, mostly advisers including about 1,200 Special Forces; about 385,000 by the end of 1966; the Army Reserve not called in 1965 or 1966 because Johnson feared the political cost
[S9] "Leading Edge of Counterinsurgency" — HistoryNet, https://historynet.com/leading-edge-counterinsurgency/ — mobile advisory teams from late 1967, five men each, 354 authorized and more than 300 operating; advisory teams in all 44 provinces; two-week in-country adviser school at Di An; six-week MATA course at Fort Bragg from 1969 including Vietnamese language; some advisers with a 12-week Defense Language Institute course; advisers removed for failing to adapt
[S10] "Army Security Force Assistance Brigades (SFABs)" — Congressional Research Service IF10675, March 2021, https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2021-03-25_IF10675_5f7d309dbda8030e73030dbd05bc4ea3a892a340.html — about 800 personnel in 36 advisor teams of 12 advisers and 8 security personnel; a one-star commander; volunteers preferred; leaders who have succeeded in the same post in a BCT; $5,000 enlistment incentive; six-week MATA course at Fort Benning; language training up to 16 weeks; foreign weapons, advanced medical, driver and SERE training; 1st SFAB attrition up to 70%, about 20% never deployed, billets filled to the day of departure; advisory duty seen as not career-enhancing
[S11] "Military Advisor Training Academy trains first SFAB" — U.S. Army, https://www.army.mil/article/197404 — MATA courses for the 1st SFAB from 7 August 2017; about 525 soldiers trained; language, rapport-building, cross-cultural communication and advising content
