# Sources — Memorandum 007

One line per source, numbered in order of first use. Cite as [S1], [S2] … in the text.

[S1] "RVNAF Leadership" — GlobalSecurity.org, globalsecurity.org/military/world/vietnam/rvn-af-leadership.htm (drawing on Clarke, *Advice and Support: The Final Years*, CMH) — RVNAF promotion system (seniority, JGS Promotion Council, annual boards, 1965 reform and 1968–71 changes), ninth-grade diploma for major, 2,653 senior officers added 1968–69, 63% fill of senior grades, captain-led battalions (37% in Dec 1970; 46 of 133 in May 1971), 1,055 reserve officers recalled 1968, field-grade command and staff schools at Dalat from 1967 required for promotion, 8,000+ officers and men at US schools 1957–69, poor leadership as advisers' foremost reason for ineffectiveness
[S2] "Thủ Đức Military Academy" — Wikipedia — founding 1951; largest source of ARVN officers; over 40,900 graduates 1952–Nov 1969; officer candidate course cut from 37 to 24 weeks in June 1968
[S3] "Vietnamese National Military Academy" — Wikipedia — Dalat course lengths (one year 1955, two years 1956, three-year classes under Diem, two years from 1963, four years from 1967); fewer than 200 officers a year before the four-year course; classes of 250; first four-year class of 92 in Dec 1969; about 1 in 10 applicants admitted in 1968
[S4] Cao Van Vien, *Leadership*, Indochina Monographs (US Army Center of Military History, 1981), DTIC ADA324491 — Diem-era promotion by confidential list decided by the President, loyalty as the basis of command, Dalat acceptance about 2 in 10, reserve officer course of nine months requiring the baccalaureate, his judgment that the lack of leaders was the RVNAF's central problem
[S5] Hoang Ngoc Lung, *Strategy and Tactics*, Indochina Monographs (CMH, 1983), DTIC ADA324506 — corps and division commanders appointed mainly on personal loyalty; coup attempts of 1960, 1963 and 1964 leading leaders to appoint and supervise key commanders personally
[S6] "Basic Officer Leaders Course" — Wikipedia — BOLC A (pre-commissioning) and BOLC B (branch) structure and locations; separation if BOLC B is not completed within two years of commissioning; BOLC II merged into BOLC B in 2010
[S7] "Changes to promotion process provide officers more career flexibility" — army.mil, article 232809 (2020) — completion of the Captains Career Course required of captains seeking early promotion; key developmental requirements under DA PAM 600-3
[S8] "Command and General Staff Officer Course (resident)" — Army University / CGSC, resources.armywarcollege.edu/cgsc/cgsoc-resident.htm — 10-month resident course at Fort Leavenworth; Common Core, Advanced Operations Course, electives
[S9] "Officer Training" — goarmy.com — Officer Candidate School of 12 weeks; BOLC as soldier skills, leadership and branch training
[S10] "Expeditionary Warfare School" — Marine Corps University, usmcu.edu/ews — 41-week resident course for captains; conference groups of about 16
[S11] "The Basic School" — Training Command, USMC, trngcmd.marines.mil/tbs — mission to train newly commissioned officers as company-grade officers, with emphasis on the rifle platoon commander
[S12] "Captains Career Course Modernization FAQ" — Army University, armyuniversity.edu (v6) — a 75-hour distributed-learning phase (C5 DL) before the resident phase; resident lengths unchanged; progression through PME per DA PAM 600-3
