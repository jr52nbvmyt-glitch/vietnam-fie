# Sources — Memorandum 017

One line per source, numbered in order of first use. Cite as [S1], [S2] … in the text.

[S1] "Intelligence Support Activity" — Wikiwand (Wikipedia mirror), https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Intelligence_Support_Activity, drawing on Sean Naylor, *Not a Good Day to Die* and *Relentless Strike* — Field Operations Group formed summer 1980 after Eagle Claw under Col. Jerry King; made permanent as USAISA on 3 March 1981; INSCOM subordination; transfer to JSOC in 2003 (Mission Support Activity); names including Task Force Orange, Gray Fox, Centra Spike; at least three squadrons (Operations, SIGINT, Mission Support/Communications) with analysis, medical and logistics detachments; growth from about 50 to about 100 in 1981; initial budget $7 million; selection by assessment course, background investigation and psychological testing; training in infiltration, advanced air operations, driving, deep surveillance, tradecraft, SIGINT; languages not required at entry but needed for field work; Dozier (Dec 1981–28 Jan 1982) SIGINT team, aerial and ground SIGINT; Queens Hunter in El Salvador from 1982 with Seaspray's Beechcraft King Air 100, planned for one month, ran more than three years; Anaconda 2002; the search for Saddam Hussein with Task Force 20 (2003); intelligence support to the Abbottabad raid (2011)
[S2] "The Intelligence Support Activity – one of America's most secretive special operations units" — Sandboxx, https://www.sandboxx.us/news/the-intelligence-support-activity-one-of-americas-most-secretive-special-operations-units — origin in the Gast memorandum's "serious and persistent information deficiency"; about 50 at founding, quickly over 100; operators drawn mainly from Army Special Forces, many of diverse cultural backgrounds; HUMINT and SIGINT mission; civilian clothes; close working relationship with the CIA; one of JSOC's Tier 1 units
[S3] "Direction Finding Goes Airborne" — EC-47 Association, https://ec47.com/direction-finding-goes-airborne — ARDF in Vietnam from late March 1962 with U-6 Beavers carrying the system that became the AN/ARD-15, as the Vietnam Flight Detachment of the 3d Radio Research Unit; six major targets fixed in the first weeks; 162 missions, 23 transmitters fixed and about 16 enemy headquarters located in the first four months
[S4] "Operation STARLIGHT: A Signals Intelligence Success Story, 15 August 1965" — US Army, https://www.army.mil/article/109115/Operation_STARLIGHT__A_Signals_Intelligence_Success_Story__15_August_1965 — 3d RRU in Vietnam from 1961; ground DF with semi-permanent sites and AN/PRD-1 teams that had to close within 5–15 miles; reasons airborne DF was needed; 30 ASA aircraft under 3d RRU by 1965; ARDF fix of the 1st VC Regiment on the Van Tuong peninsula on 15 August 1965 and Walt's attack the next day; 41 more ARDF aircraft procured afterwards and an ARDF Coordination Center formed
[S5] "Discovery of North Vietnamese Intelligence Operation" — US Vietnam War Commemoration, https://vietnamwar50th.com/1969-1971_vietnamization/Discovery-of-North-Vietnamese-Intelligence-Operation — on 20 December 1969 the 1st Infantry Division in Binh Duong (Operation TOUCHDOWN) captured 12 members of an enemy communications-reconnaissance unit, intercept equipment and thousands of documents showing a large, competent network intercepting and analysing allied communications throughout the South
[S6] "Guys Reading Mail" — HistoryNet, https://historynet.com/guys-reading-mail/ — the enemy routinely monitoring American transmissions by 1968; the December 1969 capture near Ben Suc with American radios and intercept manuals; the enemy's English linguists; the X-Mode scrambler worked with the vehicle AN/VRC-46 but not the manpack AN/PRC-25, so forward observers transmitted in the clear; Abrams: "These guys are reading our mail"
