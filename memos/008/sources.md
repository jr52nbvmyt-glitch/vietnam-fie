# Sources — memorandum 008

[S1] Economy of the Republic of Vietnam — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_Republic_of_Vietnam — industry about 10% of GDP; 85% of firms and 90% of manufacturing output in Saigon, Bien Hoa and Gia Dinh; French-era machinery shops; import-substitution policy with priority for machinery and metal imports; iron and steel among pre-1965 imports; 1973 industrial workforce 111,964 and textile workforce 32,489; Bunker's 1970 report of over $750 million imported against at most $15 million exported; industry stalled in 1968 and 1972 and fell over 20% in 1973 and 1974; the An Hao paper factory at Bien Hoa (1961).
[S2] POSCO — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/POSCO — founded April 1968; construction from 1 April 1970; Pohang works dedicated 3 July 1973 with 1.03 million t/yr; financed by $119 million in Japanese grants and loans and $54 million Export-Import Bank of Japan credit; Nippon Steel technical assistance; four phases to February 1981.
[S3] Defense industry of South Korea — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_industry_of_South_Korea — Agency for Defense Development founded 1970; licensed Colt M16 production from 1971; Heavy and Chemical Industry plan 1973 naming steel, machinery, electronics; Yulgok plan 1974; Hawk and Nike Hercules maintenance by GoldStar Precision from 1972; Special Law on Military Procurement with draft exemption for skilled workers.
[S4] "The First Military Weapon Parts Came from the 'Cheonggyecheon Scrap Dealer'" — Asia Economy (asiae.co.kr), 24 Apr 2020, https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2020042407455773604 — the 1971 ADD crash programme (directive of 11 Nov 1971, prototypes test-fired late Dec 1971); no special-steel plants, materials bought from scrap dealers; reverse-engineering of worn samples; M16 plant completed 1972 with slow efficiency gains blamed on lack of ultra-precision technology; ten-year plan for domestic small arms and ammunition by 1976.
[S5] "The Development of Korea's Electronics Industry during its Formative Years (1966–1979)" — KDI K-Developedia, https://kdevelopedia.org/k-db-originals/the-development-of-koreas-electronics-industry-during-its-formative-years-1966-1979 — GoldStar's first Korean radio, 1959; electronics exports $3.6 million in 1966; target $100 million by 1971; Electronics Industry Promotion Law 1969; consumer market shares 1969–71.
[S6] AN/PRC-77 — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AN/PRC-77 — 53.00–75.95 MHz high band; entered service 1968 as the PRC-25's successor; transistor final amplifier replacing the PRC-25's single tube and its DC-DC converter; compatible with TSEC/KY-38 NESTOR; licensed production by AEG Telefunken do Brasil in 1970; battery family BA-386 (zinc-carbon, obsolete) to BA-5598.
[S7] "Vietnam: AN/PRC-25 radio" — HistoryNet, https://historynet.com/vietnam-an-prc-25-radio/ — developed in the late 1950s to replace the AN/PRC-10, entered Vietnam in 1965; solid-state circuitry; battery two to three hours of heavy use, several days sparing; 3-foot and 10-foot antennas; Abrams's "single most important tactical field item"; spent batteries destroyed because the enemy used them in booby traps.
[S8] "RVN Forces — Logistics Self-Sufficiency by Mid-1973" — US Army (army.mil), 1970 Army Logistician feature, https://www.army.mil/article/286444/rvn_forces_logistics_self_sufficiency_by_mid_1973 — five area logistics commands; national depots in Saigon for each technical service; an 18-month $30.6 million programme to upgrade the rebuild depots, due mid-1971; some repairs still done out of country; airlift still needed; about 1,100 more depot civilians needed.
