# Sources — Memorandum 013

One line per source, numbered in order of first use. Cite as [S1], [S2] … in the text.

[S1] Memorandum From the Joint Chiefs of Staff to the Secretary of Defense, JCSM-746-64, "Recommended Courses of Action—Southeast Asia", 27 August 1964 — Foreign Relations of the United States 1964–68, vol. I, doc. 331, https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1964-68v01/d331 — the Target Study for North Vietnam of 94 targets (Appendix A to JCSM-729-64 of 24 August 1964) in five categories: airfields, lines of communication, military installations, industrial installations and routes of armed reconnaissance; the Chiefs' own caution that the programme "will not necessarily provide decisive end results"
[S2] W. Hays Parks, "Rolling Thunder and the Law of War", Air University Review, January–February 1982 (vol. XXXIII no. 2), https://sites.duke.edu/lawfire/files/2022/02/RollingThunderHaysParksAir-University-Review_-January-February-1982-Volume-XXXIII-No.-2.pdf — the Hanoi restricted zone of 30 miles and prohibited zone of 10 miles; Haiphong's 10 and 4 miles; the Chinese buffer of 30 miles west of 106° E and 25 miles east of it; Phuc Yen's prohibited circle from July 1965 to 24 October 1967; the White House rejection of the 94-target list; 1,000 of 106,000 sorties in 1966 against the 22 authorised fixed targets; the 37-day Christmas halt, 27 December 1965 – 30 January 1966; weekly then monthly target packages chosen at the Tuesday lunch; SAM sites authorised only when shown occupied; six phases and seven bombing halts
[S3] John T. Correll, "Rolling Thunder", Air Force Magazine, March 2005, https://www.airandspaceforces.com/article/0305thunder/ — the directive of 13 February 1965 for "measured and limited air action"; the first mission of 2 March 1965 against Xom Bang and Quang Khe; the first SAM site detected 5 April 1965; the halt of 12 May 1965; MiG bases barred for two years; SAM sites attackable only if they fired first; 30-nautical-mile Chinese buffer in the northwest; Hanoi and Haiphong targets not approved until October and November 1965; the POL strikes of 29 June 1966; 304,000 fighter and 2,380 B-52 sorties; 5,795 guns in 1,158 sites by 1968; one pilot lost in every 40 sorties against heavily defended areas in 1967
[S4] National Museum of the United States Air Force, "Sanctuaries and Bombing Halts" (fact sheet), https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Visit/Museum-Exhibits/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/196022/space-gallery/ — the sanctuaries around Hanoi and Haiphong and the Chinese buffer; airfields, SAM sites and POL off limits early; Hanoi–Haiphong SAM sites barred for fear of killing Soviet or Chinese technicians; 56 SAM sites located by the end of 1965; the halts of May 1965 (six days), 24 December 1965 – 30 January 1966, 31 March 1968 (north of the 20th parallel as the museum gives it) and 31 October 1968; MiG-21s flown in September 1966 from five air bases kept off limits; four of five MiG airfields released in April 1967
[S5] GlobalSecurity.org, "North Vietnamese Air Defense — Anti-Aircraft Artillery", https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/vietnam/nva-ad-aaa.htm — about 700 anti-aircraft weapons and 20 early-warning radars in 1964; 1,400 guns, 22 early-warning and four fire-control radars in September 1964; about 2,100 anti-aircraft weapons by February 1965
[S6] "Operation Rolling Thunder" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Rolling_Thunder — CINCPAC's eight-week, three-stage campaign plan begun in March 1964 and published as OPLAN 37-64 with the 94-target list at the end of August 1964; approval on 13 February 1965; the VPAF's 36 MiG-17s and about as many qualified pilots in 1965 (53 MiG-17s originally); about 1,500 anti-aircraft weapons at the opening and more than 5,000 within a year; about 150 SA-2 launchers in 25 battalions by 1967; 95 SA-2 systems and 7,658 missiles delivered by the USSR; sorties and tonnage to 24 December 1965; 180 aircraft lost to 24 December 1965; a third of imports by rail from China and two-thirds by sea through Haiphong and the other ports; the CIA's estimate of 52,000 casualties including 21,000 deaths to April 1967
[S7] John T. Correll, "Desert Storm", Air Force Magazine, January 2011, https://www.airandspaceforces.com/article/0111storm/ — the opening night of 16–17 January 1991 against communications buildings, sector operations centres and airfield radars, with F-117s on the control nodes and downtown Baghdad; more than 66 HARMs launched in an hour after the decoy wave; four US aircraft lost on the first night; more than 43,000 strike sorties in the six weeks
