# Sources — Memorandum 002

One line per source, numbered in order of first use. Cite as [S1], [S2] … in the text.

[S1] "Republic of Vietnam National Police" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Vietnam_National_Police — national decree of June 1962 merging the security services into the Directorate General of National Police; initial strength about 16,000; the US goal of 3 police per 1,000 people met by mid-1965; peak of 103,859 in February 1971, of whom only 27,565 career status; authorised about 130,000 in 1973; Elementary Training Centre at Rach Dua (12-week basic course), National Police Academy at Thu Duc; early weapons (M1 and M2 carbines, M1 Garand, M3 and Thompson submachine guns, revolvers); M16A1 from 1969; no crew-served or indirect-fire weapons
[S2] J. Grey, report for the Department of Veterans' Affairs, Advisory AN07 "Popular, Regional and National Police Field Forces in Vietnam" (19 May 1999; advisory 31 May 2000) — https://clik.dva.gov.au/book/export/html/21587 — National Police from 19,000 to 88,000 between 1964 and 1970, expansion begun in 1964; Field Force designed as a lightly armed, highly mobile formation to attack the VC in the villages; RF under the province chief and PF under the district; RF/PF over 300,000 in 1968; RF/PF caused 12–30% of enemy combat deaths for 2–4% of the resources; five-week RF/PF course
[S3] "World: Powerful White Mice", *Time*, 10 Dec 1965 — https://time.com/archive/6833379/world-powerful-white-mice/ — National Police doubled from 22,000 to 53,000; 72,000 planned for 1967 and perhaps 100,000 later; 144 American policemen of AID's Public Safety Division; Colonel Pham Van Lieu as chief; resource control on the Malayan model (highway and waterway checkpoints, mobile roadblocks, family census sweeps); identity cards for all over 18; sweeps produced 4,813 VC and 1,733 deserters; one week's seizures of 54 tons of food and 340 weapons
[S4] "South Vietnam — National Police" — GlobalSecurity.org, https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/vietnam/rvn-national-police.htm — from about 16,000 in 1963 to 90,000 in 1971; held below 80,000 in 1968 by attrition, mobilisation and funding; 13,000 ARVN transferred to the police by Thieu in mid-1969; Ministry of Interior, operationally decentralised to 44 provinces, 250 districts and six autonomous cities; province and most district chiefs military officers; Field Force created January 1966; Marine Police from 1965; 818 police killed in the Tet offensive of 1968; 12-week basic and 8-week Field Force courses; police services extended to villages during 1969; Phung Hoang estimate of 80,000 VCI and 13,404 removed in its first 11 months
[S5] "Republic of Vietnam National Police Field Force" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Vietnam_National_Police_Field_Force — created 27 January 1966; company of a 24-man headquarters and 40-man platoons of four 10-man squads; one company per province with 2–13 platoons until 1968; 16,500 men in 44 provincial battalions by August 1971; platoon weapons including the M79 and a .30-calibre machine gun, 24 shotguns per company; no mortars; officers and NCOs trained 8 weeks in Malaysia and the Philippines
[S6] "Michigan State University Vietnam Advisory Group" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University_Vietnam_Advisory_Group — police advisers peaked at 33; national identity card programme launched 1959; Diem used the Sûreté's identification registry against dissenters; Civil Guard of 60,000; MSUG and MAAG disagreement over a police-style Civil Guard; National Institute of Administration founded at Dalat in January 1953, moved to Saigon as a four-year programme; contract of $25 million over seven years
[S7] G. Shaw, *The Lost Mandate of Heaven: The American Betrayal of Ngo Dinh Diem* (Ignatius, 2015), ch. 8 "Policemen versus Soldiers" — https://erenow.org/biographies/the-lost-mandate-of-heaven-the-american-betrayal-of-ngo-dinh-diem-president-of-vietnam/8.php — Thompson's report of November 1961: police primacy in population security, intelligence in a single organisation (ideally the police special branch); Malayan police of 11,285 in 1948 grown to 30,000 regulars and 30,000 special constables, kept at more than twice the army; at least eighty security organisations in the RVN; McGarr's and Lemnitzer's objections
[S8] Memorandum from the Deputy Director of the Bureau of Intelligence and Research (Denney), 30 Jan 1964, *FRUS 1964–68* vol. I, doc. 20 — https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1964-68v01/d20 — the head of the National Police, General Mai Huu Xuan, arrested in Khanh's coup
[S9] Memorandum for the Record by Maj. Gen. W. E. DePuy, 17 Jan 1968, *FRUS 1964–68* vol. VI, doc. 16 — https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1964-68v06/d16 — "Corrupt Province Chiefs have not been removed"; province chief designees not sent to the Vung Tau school, to receive a watered-down course in Saigon instead
[S10] "How goes Thieu's government?", *Time*, 16 Aug 1968 — https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,838548-2,00.html — province chiefs appointed by the President and central to the patronage system; Thieu had sacked 16, competent replacements hard to find; $425 to become a secret-police agent and $250 to join the Regional Forces as draft havens
[S11] R. W. Komer, *Bureaucracy Does Its Thing: Institutional Constraints on U.S.–GVN Performance in Vietnam*, RAND R-967-ARPA (1972) — https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/AD0770405.pdf — CORDS collected dossiers on incompetent or venal province and district chiefs and pressed for their removal, with a "respectable" batting average 1967–72; withdrawal of support from misused Police Field Force companies forced corrective action; reform measures of 1965–67 "more promise than performance"
[S12] "RVN Corps / Military Regions" — GlobalSecurity.org, https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/vietnam/rvn-arvn-corps.htm — 44 provinces and 243 districts; province chiefs usually ARVN lieutenant colonels or colonels commanding the RF and responsible for civil administration, appointed by Saigon rather than the corps commanders by the late 1960s; district chiefs captains or occasionally majors directing RF/PF and civil activities; village chiefs elected
[S13] "Understanding the Failure of the US Security Transfer during the Vietnam War", *Journal of Indo-Pacific Affairs* (Air University) — https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/JIPA/Display/Article/3533521/understanding-the-failure-of-the-us-security-transfer-during-the-vietnam-war/ — Thieu consistently appointed political loyalists who used their posts for personal gain (citing Talmadge, *The Dictator's Army*); Snepp on General Nguyen Van Toan filling provincial posts in MR 3 "on the basis of various personal gratuities"
[S14] "Phoenix Program" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Program — 81,740 VCI "neutralised" 1968–72, of whom 26,369 killed; 87% of those killed died in conventional military operations; quotas led to fabrication and false arrest; officials took bribes to release suspects; a rule requiring three separate sources of evidence per target
[S15] Centre for Public Impact, "Seizing the moment: rebuilding Georgia's police" — https://www.centreforpublicimpact.org/case-study/siezing-moment-rebuilding-georgias-police/ — traffic police abolished July 2004; about 85% of police (about 15,000) dismissed; about $4.7 million on equipment; one law-enforcement officer per 78 citizens before reform and one per 214 in 2006; the Ministry of Internal Affairs third most trusted institution in 2009; Georgia 124th of 133 on the corruption index in 2003 and 48th of 168 in 2015; expedited training and little in-service training
[S16] "Independent Commission on Policing for Northern Ireland" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Commission_on_Policing_for_Northern_Ireland — Patten Report of 9 Sep 1999 with 175 recommendations; temporary 50:50 recruitment under the Police (NI) Act 2000; Catholic share of officers 8.3% in December 1998, 29.7% in March 2011; Policing Board and Police Ombudsman
[S17] "Indian Administrative Service" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service — 4,926 officers, two-thirds by direct recruitment; about 180 places a year from over a million applicants through the Civil Services Examination; training at the Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy of Administration; probation as sub-divisional magistrate before district collector; punitive transfers (one officer transferred more than 50 times) as the instrument of political interference
[S18] "Briggs Plan" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Briggs_Plan — in force from April 1950; about 500,000 people resettled into about 450 new villages of about 1,000 each; police guarded the settlements; food denial and curfews
[S19] US Army War College / Joint Staff, "Malaya 1947–60" case study (April 2025) — https://media.defense.gov/2025/Apr/08/2003685553/-1/-1/0/20250407_MALAYA_1947-60_FINAL.PDF — the police numbered about 10,000 at the start of the Emergency; identity cards mandated for all civilians; Briggs appointed Director of Operations in April 1950; Templer from February 1952
