# Sources — Memorandum 004

One line per source, numbered in order of first use. Cite as [S1], [S2] … in the text.

[S1] "Upper Receiver Group – Improved, Geissele's Perfect M4" — GAT Daily, 9 October 2018, https://gatdaily.com/?p=103213 — Geissele contract with USSOCOM for an upgraded M4 upper; barrel optimised for M855A1; longer mid-length gas system with mildly overbored port for reliability and smoother recoil; Mk16 13.5-inch rail with M-LOK; SureFire four-prong flash hider for suppressor interface; DDC Airborne ambidextrous charging handle; "isn't the lightest"
[S2] "Geissele URG-I Near Clone Upper" — RifleConfigurator catalogue, https://www.rifleconfigurator.com/catalog/uppers/geissele-urgi-near-clone-upper — fielded URG-I reference: Daniel Defense 14.5-inch government-profile barrel, 1:7 twist, mid-length direct impingement, 13.5-inch Mk16 M-LOK rail, SureFire SF4P pinned and welded; URG-I replaced SOPMOD Block II carbines across US Army special operations; commercial near-clone upper weighs 4 lb 3 oz
[S3] "CompM5 2 MOA Red Dot Reflex Sight" — Aimpoint AB, https://www.aimpoint.com/products/red-dot-sights/compm5-2-moa-red-dot-reflex-sight — 2 MOA dot; 148 g sight only; 50,000 hours (over five years) of constant operation; four night-vision-compatible and six daylight settings; submersible to 45 m; compatible with 3XMag-1 and 6XMag-1 magnifiers
[S4] "New M110A2 Sniper Rifle Used In Army Ranger Competition" — The War Zone, April 2022, https://www.twz.com/new-knights-armament-m110a2-rifle-used-in-army-ranger-competition — KAC M110A2, newest version of the M110 SASS, SR-25 lineage; 7.62x51mm; 20-inch free-floated match barrel; overall length 39.75–43.5 in; 10.5 lb default configuration; upgraded integral suppressor; 12-inch M-LOK handguard; ambidextrous controls; precision fire on point targets past 1,000 m; shown at AUSA October 2021; used at the Best Ranger Competition 8–11 April 2022; KAC supplied the M110 2007–2013; open IDIQ contract of $4.6 million at Picatinny; M110A1 (HK) about 10.8 lb empty and 13.7 lb loaded with the TANGO6 1-6x; CSASS contract $44.5 million for at least 3,643 rifles
[S5] "TANGO6T 1-6x24mm" — SIG Sauer, https://www.sigsauer.com/tango6t-1-6x24-mm.html — 30 mm tube; FFP about 626 g, SFP about 590 g; reticles DWLR-556, 762 Extended Range and others in 0.2 mrad; nine daylight and two night-vision illumination settings, locking dial; one CR2032; eye relief about 3.9–4.1 in; field of view about 106 ft at 100 yd at 1x and about 21–22 ft at 6x; IPX-8 to 20 m; selected by the US Army for the Squad Designated Marksman Rifle and for Special Forces
[S6] "M249 light machine gun" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M249 — 7.5 kg empty, 10 kg with 200-round pouch; 465 mm and 521 mm barrels; cyclic about 750–850 rpm normal gas; rapid about 100 rpm, sustained about 50 rpm; quick-change barrel; effective range 700 m (465 mm) or 800 m (521 mm) point, 3,600 m maximum; M27 links in 100- and 200-round pouches or STANAG magazines; PIP plastic stock, hydraulic buffer, single gas setting; Para 348 mm barrel and 7.1 kg; adopted February 1982, Army 1984, Marine Corps 1985; 2002 Natick and 2006 CNA surveys of maintenance and stoppage complaints; M250 selected April 2022; M27 IAR partial replacement in the Marine Corps from 2010
[S7] "South Vietnam — New Equipment" — GlobalSecurity.org, https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/vietnam/rvn-af-equipment.htm — MACV request for 170,000 M16s in fall 1965, reduced to 100,000; December 1966 deferral of ARVN and ROK issue; first ARVN shipments April 1967; only General Reserve airborne and Marine battalions until 1968; all ARVN infantry battalions by mid-1968; territorial forces rearmed with M16, M60, M79 and LAW, expected complete end of 1969; about 350 Mobile Advisory Teams; enemy introduced the AK-47 in 1964, with RPD, AK-47 and B-40/B-41 in infiltrating units 1965–66; ARVN units with M16 much more aggressive; M60, M79 and LAW to ARVN infantry by mid-1968
[S8] S. L. A. Marshall, "Vietnam: The Short-Range Shooting War" — American Rifleman (reprint), 24 September 2020, https://www.americanrifleman.org/articles/2020/9/24/vietnam-the-short-range-shooting-war/ — most engagements at about 10–30 m and almost never beyond 100 m; seeing-sensing distance in tropical forest about 20–30 m; enemy holding fire until scouts within 25–30 m; surprise targets at 7–15 m; rifle bullets over 70% of casualties on both sides; M16 outscoring the M60 better than two to one in estimated kills; under 20% of South Vietnam true primary jungle; three M16 kills beyond 100 m recorded; rifleman missing about four times in five in surprise encounters (author's estimates from his field notes)
[S9] "RVNAF Strength" — GlobalSecurity.org, https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/vietnam/rvn-af-strength.htm — 1964: ARVN 220,000, VNAF 11,000, VNN 12,000, VNMC 7,000, regular 250,000, RF 96,000, PF 168,000, territorial 264,000, total 514,000; 1967: RF 151,000, PF 149,000; 1969: RF 190,000, PF 214,000; 1971–72: ARVN 410,000, regular 516,000, RF 284,000, PF 248,000, total 1,048,000 (all approximate)
[S10] "Small Caliber Ammunition: M855A1 Enhanced Performance Round" — US Army STAND-TO!, 29 October 2013, https://www.army.mil/standto/2013-10-29 — clipped M855A1 released June 2010 for M4/M4A1 and M16, linked for the M249 December 2010; lead-free copper slug; penetrates 3/8-inch steel at about 400 m; penetrator almost twice as heavy as M855's; more consistent soft-target performance at range
