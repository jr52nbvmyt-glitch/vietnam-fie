# Sources — Memorandum 001

One line per source, numbered in order of first use. Cite as [S1], [S2] … in the text.

[S1] "Land reform in South Vietnam" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reform_in_South_Vietnam — Bao Dai decree of 1953 (15% rent); Ordinance 2 of 8 Jan 1955 (25% rent cap); Ordinance 57 of Oct 1956 (100 ha retention; purchasers repay over 6 years); 422,000 ha acquired and 244,000 ha distributed 1958–61, under 10% of 7.5 million acres cultivated; Ordinance 57 largely unused after 1960; Land to the Tiller from 26 Mar 1970, 15 ha limit, US financed $339 million of $441 million; Fall ("ammunition for howitzers"); Biggs ("too little, too late"); Prosterman as the model's author
[S2] R. L. Prosterman, "Land-to-the-Tiller in South Vietnam: The Tables Turn", *Asian Survey* 10:8 (Aug 1970), pp. 751–764 — https://staff.washington.edu/sherrye/project/documents/landToTheTiller.pdf — 1960–61 Delta census (1,175,000 farm families: 257,000 full owners, ~334,000 part-tenants, ~521,000 full tenants); Central Lowlands (403,000 of 695,000 families part-tenants, ~74,000 full tenants); average Delta rent 34% of crop, Central Lowlands 50%; Diem's retention limit about 30 times Japan, Korea and Taiwan, reaching about one in ten tenant families; LTTT design (zero retention of tenanted land, 15 ha owner-cultivated, 5 ha worship land, compensation 2.5 × annual paddy yield, 20% cash and 80% eight-year bonds at 10%, recipient limits 3 ha Delta and 1 ha Central Lowlands, land free, 15-year transfer ban, fines to 200,000 piastres); village committees issuing deeds in under a week for government land; the blanket preliminary distribution option; poor cadastral records
[S3] US General Accounting Office, *Progress and Problems of U.S. Assistance for Land Reform in Vietnam*, B-159451 (1973; data to 31 Mar 1973) — https://www.gao.gov/assets/b-159451-094103.pdf — LTTT goal of about 1 million ha to about 800,000 tenants in 3 years; titles for 1,007,217 ha to about 650,000 farmers by 26 Mar 1973; compensation 20% cash, balance in eight annual bond instalments at 10%, average about $282/ha, about $537 million through 1981; administration (Directorate General of Land Affairs, province services, Village Land Distribution Committees, 7-day public posting, aerial photographs, AID-run computer printing of titles and cheques); problems (false landlord declarations, tenant intimidation, collusion, reclassification as worship land, fees to accept applications, 235 of 295 villages in one region without registers, French records unverifiable, central-Vietnam village officials who are landowners, lack of grievance machinery); SRI 1968 rent average 34% against a 25% legal limit; US technical-assistance staff of 31 in July 1972
[S4] "Land reform in Vietnam" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reform_in_Vietnam — by end-1973, 953,000 titles and 1,198,000 ha, "nearly 40 percent" of the South's cultivated land; tenancy reported to have "practically disappeared" by 1975; living standards reported up 30–50%; over 70% of Delta farmers landless or renting; landlords reclaiming land behind government forces; LTTT confirming Viet Cong distributions; Race on land promises giving the Party "a core of activists in each village"
[S5] J. Race, *War Comes to Long An* (1972), pp. 128–130 (excerpt) — https://msuweb.montclair.edu/~furrg/vietnam/race128to130.html — the Party's land programme (rent and interest reduction, confiscation of absentee landlords' land, distribution to poor and landless peasants), "provisional" distribution that peasants took as permanent, Party taxes sometimes five to seven times government taxes, and the logic that only by winning the war could the peasant keep the land
[S6] D. A. Conrad, "'Before It Is Too Late': Land Reform in South Vietnam, 1956–1968", *Journal of American-East Asian Relations* 21:1 (2014), pp. 34–57 — https://www.cooperative-individualism.org/conrad-david_land-reform-in-south-vietnam-2014.pdf — under Ordinance 57 less than a third of tenanted acreage was available for redistribution; Ladejinsky on tenants' "lukewarm attitude"; Bredo's judgment that the 1970 law was "several years too late"
[S7] T. Misawa, "Agrarian Reform, Employment and Rural Incomes in Japan", *International Labour Review* 103:4 (1971), pp. 393–413 — https://webapps.ilo.org/public/libdoc/ilo/P/09602/09602(1971-103-4)393-413.pdf — tenant-cultivated share of arable land 46.2% (1941), 39.5% (1947), 9.0% (1955); tenant farmers 43.5% (1947) to 11.8% (1950); retention 1 ha for resident landlords (4 ha Hokkaido); law of Oct 1946, programme almost complete by 1950; compensation eroded by inflation
[S8] P. Gillingham and F. Buckle, *Rwanda Land Tenure Regularisation Case Study*, HTSPE / Evidence on Demand (Mar 2014) — https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/57a089b6e5274a27b2000211/EoD_HD66_March14_Rwanda_case_study.pdf — 10.3 million parcels demarcated and adjudicated Feb 2010 – Jun 2012; 8.4 million titles prepared; £3.42–4.05 ($5.47–6.48) per parcel; aerial photography index map; general-boundary demarcation by community; 60-day objection period; about 110,000 people employed, 99% local; para-surveyors trained by para-surveyors; 19% not approved, mostly for incomplete information; sustainability of later registration not secured
[S9] D. A. Ali, K. Deininger and M. Duponchel, *Sustaining the Success of the Systematic Land Tenure Registration in Rwanda*, World Bank / DFID (2016) — https://www.gov.uk/dfid-research-outputs/sustaining-the-success-of-the-systematic-land-tenure-registration-in-rwanda — 11.4 of an estimated 11.5 million parcels demarcated in under three years at under $6 a parcel, from aerial photography or satellite imagery, participatively
[S10] Vexcel Imaging, *UltraCam Eagle Mark 3* data sheet — https://www.web.geofly.eu/images/downloads/datenblaetter_sensoren/UC_Eagle_M3_en.pdf — panchromatic frame 26,460 × 17,004 pixels at 4.0 µm; 10 cm ground sample distance at about 2,000 m (f80) to 5,250 m (f210); one frame per 1.5 s; 10 TB disk pack of up to 4,600 images; camera about 61 kg; designed to work with a GNSS/INS system
[S11] Landesa, programme history page — https://landesa.org/?p=579 — the advocate's own claims for LTTT 1970–73: about 1 million tenant farmers, rice production up 30%, Viet Cong recruitment in the South cut by 80%; self-reported by the programme's designers' organisation
[S12] "Independent Commission Against Corruption (Hong Kong)" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Commission_Against_Corruption_(Hong_Kong) — founded 15 Feb 1974 after the Godber flight; Operations (about 72% of staff), Corruption Prevention and Community Relations departments; powers to arrest, search and seize; answerable only to the Governor; 1,213 staff (2007); partial amnesty for pre-1977 offences after police stormed ICAC offices; 1978 purge of 119 officers; Operations Review Committee of public members
[S13] J. Mulholland and A. Sanit, "The weakening of Indonesia's Corruption Eradication Commission", East Asia Forum (Jan 2020) — https://www.eastasiaforum.org/2020/01/28/the-weakening-of-indonesias-corruption-eradication-commission — KPK founded 2003; prosecutions reaching the president's allies and the ruling party's leadership; the 2019 revision placing a supervisory board over the KPK and subjecting surveillance to its approval, produced by a convergence of rival political leaders against it
[S14] SIGAR, *Afghan Personnel and Pay System*, SIGAR 22-34-AR (Jul 2022) — https://sigar.mil/Portals/147/Files/Reports/Audits-and-Inspections/Performance-Audits/SIGAR-22-34-AR.pdf — $64.8 million spent on the APPS contract; APPS never interfaced electronically with the biometric database; 7,714 APPS records absent from the biometric database, with at least $11.3 million paid to them; 7,100 duplicate and 1,009 invalid ID numbers in 175,195 records; DOD's claim of over 20,000 removed by biometric revalidation, undocumented; payments through trusted agents
[S15] Reuters, "Afghan troop numbers drop sharply in crackdown on 'ghost' soldiers", 1 Aug 2019 — https://sg.news.yahoo.com/afghan-troop-numbers-drop-sharply-041650157.html — a fall of about 42,000 in reported strength when reporting moved from head-counts by field officials to biometrically validated personnel
[S16] "Ghost soldiers" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_soldiers — Iraq, Nov 2014: 50,000 ghost soldiers announced by Prime Minister al-Abadi, about $360 million a year; Afghanistan: at least 40% of names on the ANA roster in Helmand nonexistent (2016); the mechanism, including in the ARVN, of keeping the dead and deserted on the rolls and drawing their pay and rice
[S17] B. Deepe, "Corruption — Hottest Saigon Issue", *New York Herald Tribune*, 1 Jun 1965, p. 12 — https://archives-spec.unl.edu/keever/item/15320 — 4–5 piastres per dollar paid for import licences; ministers paid off after each coup; 50,000 piastres for a battalion commander's transfer to Saigon; 50,000 piastres for a draft-exemption certificate; padded receipts; skimming of war widows' allotments; phantom troops whose pay went to commanders; a general and an admiral suspended
[S18] "'Economic Criminal' Shot in Saigon", *The Press* (Christchurch), 15 Mar 1966, p. 17 — https://dynamic-rsnz.natlib.govt.nz/newspapers/CHP19660315.2.159 — Ta Vinh, 35, a Chinese businessman, shot by a military firing squad in Saigon on 14 Mar 1966 after a one-day trial by the special court for economic crimes, for speculation, hoarding and bribery
[S19] "How goes Thieu's government?", *Time*, 16 Aug 1968 — https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,838548-2,00.html — Thieu has sacked 16 province chiefs; province chiefs as the core of the patronage system; an ARVN colonel charged with corruption not tried because of his influence; no competent replacements; draft avoidance bought for $425 (secret-police agent) or $250 (Regional Forces)
[S20] "Commercial Import Program" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_Import_Program — from Jan 1955; peak of $398 million in 1966; licensed importers buying aid dollars with piastres, the counterpart funds paying the army, police and civil service; licences withheld for bribes; a 1966 US report that a 100% domestic profit on piastres was possible
[S21] US General Accounting Office, *Survey of the Agency for International Development's Management and Operation of the Commercial Import Program for Viet Nam*, B-159451 (24 Aug 1967) — https://www.gao.gov/assets/b-159451-094015.pdf — $397 million obligated in 1966; Saigon cost of living up 40% (1965) and 68% (1966); collusion, overpricing, kickbacks and short shipment; licences split under $10,000 to avoid competitive bidding; off-loading theft estimated at 2–5% and unverifiable; end-use checks limited; importer suspensions
[S22] E. Villard, "Was the South Vietnam government corrupt?", HistoryNet (Sep 2022) — https://historynet.com/south-vietnam-corruption/ — US aid averaging about $1.5 billion a year 1965–74 against a GDP averaging about $10 billion; US officials' priority on anti-corruption 1963–67; Thieu's curbs on military corruption after Tet; corruption persisting to 1975
[S23] "RVNAF desertions" — GlobalSecurity.org, https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/vietnam/rvn-af-desertions.htm — 73,000 desertions in 1964, 113,000 in 1965; family and village loyalty; weak punishment
[S24] "RVNAF strength" — GlobalSecurity.org, https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/vietnam/rvn-af-strength.htm — 1964: regulars 250,000 (Army 220,000), Regional Forces 96,000, Popular Forces 168,000, total 514,000; 1963 MAP-supported levels 225,000 / 86,000 / 104,000
