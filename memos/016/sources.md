[S4] "The Model 1911 in Vietnam" — American Rifleman (NRA), 11 September 2020, https://www.americanrifleman.org/articles/2020/9/11/the-model-1911-in-vietnam/ — the M1911 as the tunnel rats' "principal tool", entering with a pistol and flashlight; pistols as the close-range fallback when rifles jammed or ran dry; there were "never enough" M1911s; private civilian handguns carried, more as the M16's malfunctions were rumoured; muzzle flash and blast a drawback in tunnels; ARVN use; photographs of the 25th Infantry Division's tunnel clearers at Phu Hoa Dong, the Hobo Woods and the Iron Triangle in 1967
[S5] "Tunnel rat" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunnel_rat — standard issue an M1911 or M1917 revolver with bayonet, flashlight and explosives; some preferred a suppressed .38 Special revolver; scarce suppressed High Standard .22s; private weapons from .25 automatics to sawn-off shotguns; the .45's blast could leave a man temporarily deaf underground; most went without gas masks; booby traps, collapse, flooding U-bends; Operation Crimp, January 1966; Australian 3 Field Troop 1965–66; US 1st Engineer Battalion at Cu Chi in 1969; clearers generally 165 cm or shorter
[S6] "South Vietnam — National Police" — GlobalSecurity.org, https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/vietnam/rvn-national-police.htm — National Police formed by decree June 1962; about 16,000 in 1963; strength unable to exceed 80,000 in 1968; 13,000 ARVN transferred mid-1969; about 90,000 in 1971; target 105,000 end 1970; National Police Field Force created January 1966, 16,000 assigned on 1 August 1970, 40-man platoons, M16 as primary weapon by then, M79 and a .30-calibre machine gun per platoon, 24 shotguns per company pool, no mortars
[S7] "RVNAF strength" — GlobalSecurity.org, https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/vietnam/rvn-af-strength.htm — RVNAF strength 1964: ARVN 220,000, VNAF 11,000, VNN 12,000, VNMC 7,000, regular total 250,000, RF 96,000, PF 168,000, total 514,000; 1971–72: regulars 516,000, RF 284,000, PF 248,000, total 1,048,000; intervening years
[S8] "M1911 pistol" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1911_pistol — M1911A1: about 1,100 g empty with magazine, 216 mm long, 127 mm barrel on the Government model, seven-round magazine among the family's options, a wider front sight the only sight change from the M1911; about 1.9 million M1911A1s procured in the Second World War (Remington Rand 900,000, Colt 400,000, Ithaca 400,000, Union Switch & Signal 50,000, Singer 500); production cancelled at the war's end and later needs met from stocks, many rebuilt and refinished at arsenals before reissue; widely used in Vietnam; South Vietnam a user; the Viet Cong used captured pistols and crude copies; standard issue until the Beretta 92F (M9) was adopted on 14 January 1985
