# Sources — Memorandum 014

One line per source, numbered in order of first use. Cite as [S1], [S2] … in the text.

[S1] "Delta Force" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Force — established 19 November 1977 by Charles Beckwith and Colonel Thomas Henry; "nearly 1,000 soldiers" (Naylor, 2001), of whom "about 250 to 300" trained for direct action and hostage rescue; size officially classified; assault squadrons A, B, C (c. 1990) and D (2006), E Squadron (aviation, 1989), G Squadron (advanced force operations), signal and combat support squadrons; each sabre squadron two assault troops and one reconnaissance troop, each assault troop four teams of five or six; selection four weeks twice a year, the Operator Training Course about six months, about 12–16 of 120 passing selection; organised under USASOC and controlled by JSOC; operations from Eagle Claw (1980) to the al-Baghdadi raid (26 October 2019)
[S2] "Aviation Technology Office" (Flight Concepts Division) — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_Concepts_Division — Delta's covered aviation, E Squadron; established as SEASPRAY 2 March 1981, absorbed into Delta by 1989; company-sized; unmarked modified Hughes 500D and Little Bird helicopters, Cessna and Beechcraft King Air fixed-wing aircraft (nine acquired), a stealth UH-60 variant in 2011; clandestine movement of personnel and materiel
[S3] "Military Assistance Command, Vietnam – Studies and Observations Group" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Assistance_Command,_Vietnam_–_Studies_and_Observations_Group — SOG formed 24 January 1964; end-1969 strength 1,041 Army, 476 USAF, 17 USMC, 7 CIA, 3,068 SCU, 5,402 civilians, 10,210 in all; Laos authorised 21 September 1965, first mission 18 October 1965 about 24 km inside; the ambassador limiting depth, targets and duration; Daniel Boone ordered April 1967, first mission September 1967, teams crossing on foot without tactical air, no exploitation forces; 454 Cambodian reconnaissance missions in 1969; 456 agents sent into the North 1960–68, more than three-quarters of Timberwork's captured at or soon after insertion, radios turned; northern operations halted 1968; 79 SF killed or missing in 1968, 17 killed at FOB 4 on 22–23 August 1968; Prairie Fire 1969: 20 Americans killed, 199 wounded, 9 missing; teams of three Americans and 3–12 indigenous soldiers; company-sized Hatchet forces
[S4] "Shining Brass" — sogsite.com, https://sogsite.com/?p=845 — the first missions of October–November 1965 about 15 miles (24 km) inside Laos; teams of two Americans and four Vietnamese; inserted by VNAF H-34s of the 219th Squadron; 88 strike sorties against the D-1 terminus; Captain Larry Thorne and the H-34 crew and a FAC lost on the first missions
[S5] "Operation Ivory Coast" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Ivory_Coast — Son Tay raid 21 November 1970, 37 km west of Hanoi; 56 Army Special Forces and 92 airmen; 28 aircraft in the target area, 116 in the operation; about 27 minutes on the ground; camp empty, prisoners moved 14 July 1970; no raiders killed, two wounded; planning 25 May to 20 November 1970
[S6] "Joint Personnel Recovery Center" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Personnel_Recovery_Center — formed September 1966 under SOG; recovery operations attempted 39 (1967), 32 (1968, 8 successful), 29 (1969, 9 successful); South Vietnamese recovered 155 (1968) and 117 (1969); "never recovered a single US POW"
[S7] "Central Office for South Vietnam" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Office_for_South_Vietnam — "a staff of some 2,400 people who are widely dispersed and highly mobile" (Time, 1970); no fixed headquarters; Mimot plantation in the Fishhook 1965–70; Junction City (February–March 1967) attempted to capture or destroy it; Menu struck it repeatedly without killing a leader; evacuated 19 March 1970 to Kratie before the incursion; Soviet trawlers warned of B-52 raids 1968–70; Nguyen Chi Thanh dominant to July 1967, then Pham Hung
[S8] "Joint Special Operations Command" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Special_Operations_Command — established 15 December 1980 on Beckwith's recommendation after Eagle Claw; Pope Field, Fort Bragg; subordinate task forces (Delta, DEVGRU, 24th STS, ISA, Ranger reconnaissance company, Joint Communications Unit); 160th SOAR controlled when deployed in JSOC task forces
