# Voice — what the memoranda should sound like

**Nothing in this file is part of the record.** The places, people and events below are illustrations of register, invented or simplified for the purpose. Never take a fact from this file. Read it once per thread, with RULES.md, and write to it.

---

## The difference

**Dry — do not write like this:**

> The provision strengthens SECURE and ADVISE. Its first-order effect on FIND.main_force is nil (F001-4). The binding constraint migrates from the land grievance to night security (G-2), and the counter-directional finding is that titles raise the stakes of SECURE failures.

Every word is defensible and none of it lands. It is a list of labels with verbs between them. The reader learns that something happened to some categories.

**Alive — write like this:**

> The title is a sheet of paper with a red seal, and its value depends entirely on who walks the hamlet path after dark. In a hamlet the Popular Forces hold at night, it is property: the family plants, harvests and sells, and the man who used to collect the rent is in Saigon complaining to a deputy. In a hamlet the Front holds at night, it is a list of names, and the district cadre will have a copy within the week.
>
> That is the whole problem with this reform, and also the reason it matters. We have not added a single rifle to the hamlet. We have changed what the men already there are guarding. A Popular Forces squad raised from tenants guarded a landlord's rent; the same squad raised from owners guards its own fields. I cannot put a number on that change and I will not pretend to. I am fairly sure it is the most important thing in this memorandum.

The second version makes the same argument. It puts it on the ground, follows one family through it, says what it costs, and lets the commander say what he believes.

## Analysis (Parts 1 and 2)

- **Open with the verdict, in a sentence a colonel could repeat at dinner.** Then the reasoning.
- **One concrete case per argument.** A hamlet, a district chief, a battalion, a night. If the argument cannot be shown anywhere, ask whether it is true.
- **Commit.** State the judgment once, at its real weight, with one honest qualification if one is needed — not three. "I am fairly sure" is a qualification; "it may potentially in some circumstances" is fog.
- **The enemy is a mind, not a variable.** He reads the same situation, wants something, and chooses. Say what he concludes and why he is right or wrong.
- **Numbers carry weight only when they are few.** One figure a paragraph that the reader remembers beats six they skim.

## Chronicle (Part 3, the chapters, §4.0)

- **Tell it in order.** Who moved, when, what they found, what went wrong, what it cost, what it meant.
- **Give each event a turn.** The moment the battle tipped, the decision that could have gone the other way, the report that arrived too late.
- **People decide things.** A named commander chooses; a named adviser argues; a regiment's political officer weighs whether to stand. Formations are how the record counts; people are how the reader follows.
- **End on the consequence.** The last sentence of an event says why it mattered to what came next.
- **The clock is a character.** Washington, the press, the casualty lists, the appropriation — let the reader feel the time running.

An illustrative chronicle paragraph (invented):

> The relief column left Ba Ria at first light on the 30th, two Ranger battalions in trucks with a platoon of M113s leading, and it never reached the village. The regiment had not stayed to defend Binh Gia; it had left a company there and laid its ambush on the road the relief had to use, in rubber where the carriers could not leave the track. The first carrier burned at 0820. By noon the Rangers were holding a perimeter around their own trucks and asking for helicopters that the gunfire made it impossible to land. The lesson Hanoi took from that day was not that its regiments could beat the ARVN in the field. It was that they could choose the field, and that the ARVN would keep driving down the road to it.

## The commander

COMUSMACV writes to someone he trusts with the truth. He is proud of what works and says so plainly. He is frustrated by what Washington draws on maps, and lets it show in a sentence, not a paragraph. He signs the casualty lists. He can see the clock. Let him sound like a man, not a report.
