# META — Meta-checkpoint (B3)

**Trigger:** `META` or `META-CHECKPOINT`, due after every `meta_every_checkpoints` checkpoints. Its own thread, two parts on `CONTINUE`. No new provision is introduced. This procedure carries the same authority as `rules/RULES.md`. **It is theater-specific** — its exploration dimensions are keyed to Vietnam.
**Output:** `reviews/MTnn/part1.md`, `part2.md`. Reading copies in `build/reviews/MTnn/`.

**Reading.** `python3 scripts/fie.py review MTnn --init`, then `python3 scripts/fie.py pack MTnn`. The pack holds the full Trajectory Log, every settled position and directive, the end state and its history, the gap register, and the cards since the last meta-checkpoint. Read it, then every chapter. For anything older, search with `fie.py grep` and read the lines it finds. **Say in Part 1 what you could not read** — an audit that claims to have read a record it only sampled is the failure it exists to catch.

A checkpoint audits the war. A meta-checkpoint audits the analysis, then uses what it finds to force the analysis into territory it has been avoiding. Two parts, distinct in purpose and voice.

## Part 1 — Analytical audit

Step outside the COMUSMACV voice. This is not the general assessing his war; it is an audit of the general's analysis by a reader with access to every memorandum, every checkpoint, the chapters and the registers, and no loyalty to any position taken in them. The standard is logical validity and internal consistency, not military judgment. Traverse systematically:

1. **Contradictions.** Positions held simultaneously across the body of memoranda that cannot both be true. A memorandum that claims interdiction is reducing throughput and a later one that assumes undiminished infiltration without addressing the discrepancy. Two provisions assessed as solving the same problem with different mechanisms but never reconciled. A chapter that shows the enemy weakened and an end-state call that assumes him undiminished. Name the memoranda, quote the positions, and state which must give way or whether both can be rescued by a distinction not yet drawn.
2. **Unsupported claims.** Conclusions stated without the evidence trail that would support them. An end-state call that moved a rung without the mechanism being traceable through the chapters. A chain whose links were asserted rather than demonstrated by a dated, named event. A channel effect claimed in §2 that never materialised in the war, or one that materialised without having been caught in §2. Look in both directions.
3. **Stale positions.** Judgments that were reasonable when made but which later provisions, events, or the accumulation of the force should have forced a reconsideration that never happened. A gap in the register that has since been filled but is still carried as open. An enemy capability assumed present that a provision has since degraded. An ARVN weakness assessed early that later advisory effort or equipment may have altered. Each produces either a reaffirmation with updated reasoning or a reversal.
4. **Metric corruption.** Places where the analysis relied on a metric it elsewhere acknowledged as unreliable, or treated an improving metric as evidence of progress without the causal mechanism being stated. Where the body count crept back in as evidence. Where HES scores were cited without the A13 caveat. Where an `[X-…]` judgment came to be cited as data. Where a provision was credited with an effect measured only by the metric it was designed to improve — circular evidence disguised as confirmation.
5. **Consistency of the enemy.** Does the enemy as portrayed across all memoranda behave as a coherent actor with a consistent — though evolving — doctrine, capability, and decision-making process? Or has the analysis given him whatever behaviour the current memorandum needed? Has his capability been silently degraded where the narrative needed a success, or silently restored where it needed a challenge? Has his adaptation been brilliant when it served the counter-directional finding and inept when it served the end-state call? Reconstruct the enemy's war from his side and test whether it reads as a single coherent campaign.
6. **Structural bias.** Has the analysis systematically favoured one kind of provision over another — ISR over firepower, firepower over pacification, the main-force war over the village war? Has the §2 traverse been thorough for some provisions and perfunctory for others? Have the chapters been revised in depth where the favoured provisions dominate and thinly elsewhere? The audit is looking for the analyst's habits of attention, which are invisible from inside them.
7. **The unfought fights.** Decisions the analysis avoided — provisions it could have called ineffective but didn't, constraints it could have challenged but didn't, end-state calls it hedged when it had enough to commit, interactions it noted but never resolved. Analytical courage is as important as analytical thoroughness, and the absence of a hard call is itself a finding.

Every finding is numbered **M-1, M-2, …** and carries a **disposition**:
- *reverse* — the position is changed: a `REVMTnn-k` line in a `<!-- positions -->` block naming the position reversed, the affected end-state call restated, and the chapters fixed where the war itself was wrong;
- *reaffirm* — the position survives the audit, with the updated reasoning;
- *investigate* — insufficient information to resolve: state what would resolve it, and issue a directive for the next memorandum that engages it;
- *remediate* — a standing correction to the analysis's method: a `DIRMTnn-k` line in a `<!-- directives -->` block, applied in every later memorandum.

## Part 2 — Unexplored terrain

*A fresh thread: read `reviews/MTnn/part1.md` (your Part 1) and the pack; read a chapter only where an item needs it.*

Return to the analytical voice. A systematic search for aspects of the conflict the analysis has neglected — not because they were excluded but because the memorandum structure, the provisions received, or the analyst's habits of attention let them fall below the threshold of treatment. The dimensions below are search headings, not a checklist to walk perfunctorily. For each, the question is: has the analysis treated this as an active force that shapes outcomes, or as scenery? Where it has been scenery, provide the analysis the memoranda should have contained — specific, dated, consequential, at the chronicle standard — and edit the chapters where the war itself changes.

- **The war the Vietnamese fight.** Not ARVN as a force element in your order of battle, but the war as experienced, understood, and fought by Vietnamese soldiers, administrators, and civilians on both sides. What has the analysis assumed about Vietnamese motivations, competence, political agency, and willingness to die for their causes that it has never examined? Where has "ARVN" been a monolith when it is many divisions of many qualities? Where has "the population" been passive when it is millions of people deciding daily whom to feed, whom to inform on, and whom to hide?
- **The wars within the war.** Intra-allied friction — ROK conduct in Binh Dinh, Australian doctrinal disagreements over cordon-and-search, RVNAF resentment of American methods, the advisory relationship as a source of friction rather than capability. ARVN politics — the generals, the coups that didn't happen, the promotions that were and weren't made, the corps commanders who were warlords. The Montagnard question and FULRO. The Buddhist crisis and its successors. The Hoa Hao and Cao Dai as political-military actors. The competition between COSVN and Hanoi for control of the southern war. The Sino-Soviet split as it plays out inside the Politburo and shapes the quality and quantity of aid.
- **The economic war.** Rice production, procurement, and denial — both sides. Taxation — the VCI tax system as a measure of control, the GVN tax system as a measure of legitimacy. Inflation and the piaster. The black market and the diversion of American supplies. The construction boom and the service economy around American bases — what it does to rural labour markets, to ARVN enlistment, to the social structure of Saigon. Land reform — the single most powerful pacification tool and the one most consistently sabotaged by the people it is supposed to benefit. A provision that changes the military balance without changing the economic balance may not have changed anything.
- **The calendar as strategy.** Monsoon as a strategic rhythm that determines when the enemy moves, fights, plants and harvests. The American fiscal year and the appropriation cycle. The election cycle — American and South Vietnamese. The one-year rotation and the replacement system as a structural degradation of unit quality that no provision addresses. Tet as a cultural and political event, not only a military one.
- **The geography the analysis forgot.** Provinces, districts, or regions that appear in no chapter despite being contested or strategically significant. The IV Corps delta — a different war with different enemies, terrain and force requirements. The Central Highlands beyond the Ia Drang set pieces. The coastal provinces between Da Nang and Qui Nhon. The northern I Corps provinces below the DMZ. Wherever the record is silent, state what happened there and what the silence cost — a province unexamined is a province assumed stable, and that assumption is usually wrong.
- **The people the analysis forgot.** Specific province chiefs, district senior advisors, RF/PF company commanders, VC district committee secretaries, PAVN regimental and division commanders, pilots and forward air controllers, engineers and medical officers. The memoranda name formations; the war turns on people. Where an individual's quality, judgment, courage, corruption, or failure determined an outcome the analysis attributed to a system, name that person (add them to a `<!-- persons -->` block) and state what they did.
- **The information war.** Radio Hanoi. The photograph as weapon. International opinion. PSYOP and its mirror — enemy propaganda inside the ARVN, the population, and the American force. The credibility gap as a measurable divergence between what MACV reports and what the press reports, and its effect on the clock.
- **Open search.** Anything else the audit reveals has been systematically below the analysis's threshold. Name it, describe the treatment it deserves, and state why the structure missed it.

Each item that produces substantive new analysis carries a **carriage directive**: *standing* — the dimension joins the analysis's permanent attention: a `DIRMTnn-k` line in the `<!-- directives -->` block; or *episodic* — a specific gap now filled, needing further treatment only when a provision engages it. Standing directives are the meta-checkpoint's most important output, because they change what every later memorandum sees.
