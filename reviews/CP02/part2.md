# CP02 — Part 2: Trajectory and limits

Part 1 found the war better at its floor and unchanged at its ceiling. After ten memoranda, the Republic's soldier, sergeant, lieutenant, battalion commander and adviser are all better than history's, and so are the posts that hold the held belt at night. The enemy answered each improvement by declining the improved unit and moving his war onto what no provision reached. This part takes the war forward from the force now held, names what binds it, and turns the bill into a menu.

## 8. Revised trajectory

**Phase I (1964–65)** stands as written. A state is built under the collapse at the top, and the army is taught from the private upward. It is still the historical collapse in Saigon and the historical escalation in Washington. Of the variables the provisions can move, nothing more will move in this phase. Every institution that could start in 1964 has started.

**Phase II (1965–67)** is where the force is strongest. ARVN effectiveness has stood at the cap of +3 since memorandum 007. Its battalions broken in 1966–67 are two-fifths to a half fewer than history's (POSCP02-1). The held belt is held at night, its infrastructure is driven out, and its land is titled. The main-force war is history's: the enemy chooses every battle except the siege, and the frontier battles draw the American divisions to the borders on Hanoi's schedule (EVCP02-3). A further provision on the ARVN battalion would add inside a band that cannot rise. A provision on the territorial edge, the contested belt at night, would act where Phase II still has room.

**Phase III (1968)** keeps its turn. Tet is decided in Saigon, Hue, at Khe Sanh and in Washington. The provisions make the Delta's Tet cheaper and the mobilization better led, and they send the enemy's August offensive against the self-defence groups (EV009-4, EV010-4). The American clock is spent on its historical date by its historical causes (POSCP01-1).

**Phase IV (1969–71)** is the inheritance and its capture. The handover of fire to Vietnamese hands at district level is the force's best Vietnamization (EV008-7). The palace's capture in 1970–71 of every institution built behind the American lever is the force's worst loss. The NCO gate alone survived, because it threatened no one (POS010-2). Nothing on the record now held will change that capture. It is the lever's lapse, and the lever lapses with the drawdown.

**Phase V (1972)** stays history's, with a tenth more PAVN losses at An Loc and Kontum (POSMT01-2). The 1957 officer classes use American air precisely, and the Republic holds where American air is decisive. C006-4's claim that the divisions are the least-advised part of the RVNAF in 1972 is unwritten in the chapter and is the next test (Part 1 §4).

**Phase VI (1973–75)** stays history's in its shape and its date. The arsenal stretches the last aid dollar, the schooled squads hold longer where they are attacked, and the PAVN bypasses them. The corps dissolve toward the soldiers' families (POSMT01-6), the palace's orders of 13–14 March are given as they were, and the 1st Corps marches against the rains.

**The A9 call: rung (a), Collapse. The Republic of Vietnam falls on 30 April 1975, within a window of about 27 April to the first days of May.**

**Rung (b), dependent survival, is not reached.** Nothing in the force changes the three things on which the Republic's survival without American combat forces depends: who commands the corps (G-4 above the regiment), whether the Republic owns the fire and lift on which its improved battalions' tactics rest (POSCP01-2, POS010-4), and whether the soldier stays with his unit when his family is in the offensive's path (G-17).

**The date test (DIRMT01-1).** One mechanism now pulls the date later: the better-led battalions and posts, which hold longer where the PAVN attacks them. A second is the arsenal, which keeps their guns fed (POS008-3). Two pull it earlier. The schooled squad's habit of calling American fire becomes helplessness when that fire is gone (F010-24). The palace's capture of the institutions removed the honest commanders before 1972 (POS007-3). The later-pulling mechanisms act on fights the PAVN chose to bypass in 1975. The earlier-pulling ones act on the corps commands, which decided the collapse of I and II Corps. They roughly cancel, and neither approaches the three-week delay that would put the assault into the rains (POSMT01-3). The date stands, as a conclusion of the test. `history/endstate.toml` is unchanged.

## 9. Limiting factors

Ranked by how directly each blocks rung (b):

1. **Who commands the corps and divisions** (G-4, POS007-2, POSCP01-3). This binds the war of 1972 and 1975 directly, since both were decided by corps commanders and their orders. Five memoranda improved every command below the regiment. Not one has reached the choice of the men above it, and the list that might have was advisory by 1970.
2. **Fire, air and lift the Republic owns** (G-12, G-15, POSCP01-2, POS010-4). This binds everything the improved battalion knows how to do, because its doctrine, from the 1957 classes and from the 2026 NCO course, is to fix the enemy and call fire. In 1975 the fire is not there to call.
3. **The soldier's pay and family** (G-17, POS009-3, POSMT01-6). This binds the army's cohesion in the last campaign, and it binds desertion in every year. The training, the schooled sergeant and the board's commander all go home with the deserter. Part 1 §6 found it visible from every position in the Republic below the ministry.
4. **The enemy's control of engagement** (POSTNA-3, G-7, G-8). This binds the main-force war in every phase, and every improvement of the ARVN since 004 has been answered by it (POSCP02-2).
5. **The American clock** (G-5). This binds everything after 1968. The sum of lives saved has grown to perhaps six to thirteen percent of the historical dead (Part 1 §5) and still crosses no band.
6. **The American lever and its lapse** (G-6). This binds the survival of every Vietnamese institution the provisions built. The force now holds one rule that survived without it, the NCO gate (POS010-2), and it survived because it was small.
7. **Night security outside the held belt** (G-2). This binds pacification in the contested belt. The schooled post holds better, and the hamlet outside its wire does not (POS008-4). It is ranked low because the countryside is not where 1975 was lost.

## 11. The bill

What the force still lacks, ranked by what it would do for the next rung:

1. **A choice of corps and division commanders the palace cannot make alone.** Every improvement below the regiment was decided above it. The 2026 US Army's Command Assessment Program, a multi-day assessment of cognitive, physical, psychometric and peer measures with a double-blind panel interview, would replace the board's ranked list with something harder to make advisory. It needs an American aid condition as strong as the one that held the list, and it must reach the corps, which the list never did.
2. **Pay that buys rice, and families housed away from the front.** The soldier's family is the gap the last campaign turned on, and no provision has treated it. A 2026-style indexed allowance for subsistence and housing, paid in rice or indexed to it, together with family quarters in rear garrison towns, acts on desertion in every year and on the dissolution of March 1975.
3. **Air support the Republic owns.** This stands from CP01, ungranted. The improved battalion's doctrine calls for fire, and in 1975 there is none. A light attack force the VNAF can fly and maintain is the one instrument that contests the dependence POSCP01-2 and POS010-4 describe.
4. **Artillery the Republic owns, precise enough to replace American air at the post and the besieged town.** The district guns of 1970 at Long My are the force's best handover (EV008-7). A Vietnamese 155mm artillery with precision fuzes would carry the same handover to the divisions.
5. **Lift the Republic owns.** The reaction at night and the corps reserve in 1975 both ride on helicopters. This stands from CP01, ungranted.
6. **The territorial edge at night.** Night vision for the RF/PF, from CP01, ungranted, would move the line on which land, police, rifles, radios and courses all stop. It is the instrument for the contested belt, where Phase II still has room.
7. **A measure of attacks not made.** This is not a provision of equipment. The force's best effects are invisible because no return records the attack the enemy declined. A 2026-style operations research cell that compares post-attack rates by post characteristics would make them visible. It is not on the menu because it is a method, not a provision, and I name it in the directives instead.

## 12. Phase redraw

No memorandum since CP01 proposed a redraw (006–010 §4.N each declined), and the war does not warrant one. Every boundary still marks a turn that happened on its date. `history/phases.toml` is unchanged.

## 13. Method

Part 1 shows two corrections are needed.

The first concerns size. Ten memoranda have filled Phases I and II with their own events. Consolidating the historical events, as the procedure prescribes, cut only about 2,200 words from two chapters that are 7,600 and 9,300 over the limit. The remaining excess is the memoranda's own minor events and overlapping paragraphs in the overview and ending.

The second concerns two habits that make the record look surer than it is. Chains are resolved by the memorandum that wrote them. Increments are added inside a band that can no longer move, so the band never shows them.

<!-- directives -->
- **DIRCP02-1** · At each checkpoint, consolidate a chapter over the limit in two steps: first merge minor historical events into summary events, then merge each memorandum's own minor NEW events in that phase into one summary per memorandum, keeping every fact a later memorandum or position cites by event number; and every memorandum's §4 adds to a phase's overview and ending only one paragraph, replacing rather than appending where an earlier memorandum's paragraph states the same variable (CP02 consolidation of P1 and P2, still about 7,600 and 9,300 words over the limit after the first step)
- **DIRCP02-2** · In each Part 3 the chain taken under DIRCP01-1 must be one written by another memorandum whose terminal event this memorandum did not write; C006-4, whose terminal link no chapter carries, is the first such chain for any memorandum revising Phase V (17 of 19 chains in memoranda 006–010 resolved as their authors predicted)
- **DIRCP02-3** · Where a provision acts on a variable already at the cap of the bands in a phase, the memorandum states its increment in the running sum of DIRMT01-2 with the factors it multiplies and how they overlap, and names the target the enemy moves to in answer (POSCP02-2); a product of overlapping judgments is reported as a range with its upper bound marked (CP02 calibration of the battalions-not-broken figure)
<!-- /directives -->

The settled positions from this checkpoint are in Part 1 (POSCP02-1 to POSCP02-3).
