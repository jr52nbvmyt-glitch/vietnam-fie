# MT01 — Part 1: Analytical audit after seven memoranda

This is not COMUSMACV's voice. It is an audit of his analysis, by a reader with the whole record open and no stake in any position in it, and it was called out of turn: the configuration asks for a meta-checkpoint after every third checkpoint, and there has been one. It was called because of a single question, put plainly by the reader of the series: with land reform and an honest administration, a reformed advisory corps, a war on the infrastructure, precision bombing, persistent reconnaissance and a partly reformed officer corps, how can the fall of Saigon not have moved by so much as a day?

The short answer this audit reaches is in two halves, and they point in different directions.

**The rung survives the audit. The date does not.** Rung (a), collapse, is a judgment the record supports with mechanisms traceable through the chapters: every one of the eighteen provisions is either American-owned and gone by 1973, or works below the level of command at which 1972 and 1975 were decided, or was captured by the palace when the American lever lapsed in 1970–71. None of them touches the four things the end turned on: the aid appropriation of 1974, the PAVN's sanctuaries and its rebuilding after 1972, the Republic's lack of an air force it owned, and the decisions of Thieu and his corps commanders in March 1975. That is a defensible reason for no rung to move.

But "30 April 1975" is not a judgment the record has derived. It is a fixed point the record has held. The end state file was last changed by the baseline and by nothing since. Seven memoranda, one checkpoint and roughly sixty altered events later, every Trajectory Log entry ends on the same sentence, and the final phase's chapter now contains a dozen mechanisms that act on the last campaign, some of which should make it shorter and some longer, each of which is followed by a line saying that the outcome was as it was historically. Not one memorandum has asked what date the war it had just written would produce. The reader's question is therefore a fair one, and the audit's answer is that the analysis has not yet earned the right to say "not a day".

The findings below follow the seven headings of the procedure. Each carries a disposition. The one that matters most is **M-12**, the unfought fight over the date; it is placed last because it gathers the others.

## What this audit read, and what it did not

Read in full: the meta-checkpoint pack (every causal chain from memoranda 001–007, the whole Trajectory Log, the end state and its history, every settled position and standing directive, the gap register at its latest status, the provision index, and the generated digest of the enemy's decisions); the card for memorandum 001 in full; the Phase VI chapter in full; and the end vector of every chapter.

Read in part: the cards for memoranda 002–007 (verdicts, findings registers and pushes; the employment-concept and interaction lines of the cards for 006 and 007 were not read); the Phase V chapter (its overview, its event list, its last event and its close); the Phase IV chapter's close; one passage of Phase III (the Accelerated Pacification Campaign, EV000-54); and the net-assessment sections of memoranda 001–007, searched for every sentence that discusses timing, date or schedule.

Not read: the Phase I and Phase II chapters beyond their end vectors (about 225,000 characters together); memoranda Parts 1 and 2 beyond what the cards carry; the checkpoint's Part 2. Findings that rest on those records are stated as such, and none of the twelve below depends on a passage this audit did not read. If any of them is contradicted by a passage in Phases I–II, that passage wins and the finding falls.

## 1. Contradictions

**M-1 · The 1972 sieges cost the PAVN more, and nothing after 1972 knows it.** Memorandum 005 committed, in its strongest-positive finding and in two positions, to the judgment that the measured sieges of this war became the enemy's worst battles: the siege box broke the penetration of An Loc on 12 May, held the assault on Kontum short of the wire, and made Kontum secure five days early; the Easter armour was tracked from 12 March and struck through the cloud before it crossed (F005-21, POS005-3, EV000-74, EV000-76, EV000-77). Memorandum 003 added the armour columns seen before the crossing. Yet the close of Phase V keeps the historical count of the PAVN's losses for the year, about 100,000 killed and wounded and most of its committed armour, as the historical record gives it; and Phase VI opens with Resolution 21 and the North's rebuilding of 1973–74 as an unaltered historical event (EV000-87). Both cannot stand at full weight. If the sieges were worse for the enemy than history's, the North entered 1973 with fewer trained men and fewer tanks than history's, and the corps that attacked in December 1974 were either smaller, or later, or rebuilt by a greater effort that has to be shown. If the North's rebuilding is unchanged, the 1972 sieges were not the enemy's worst battles by any margin that mattered, and the strongest-positive finding of memorandum 005 is overstated. A distinction can rescue both: the siege box may have changed *where* the PAVN's 1972 losses fell (more at An Loc and Kontum, fewer elsewhere) without changing their total. That distinction has not been drawn. **Disposition: investigate.** What would resolve it: a statement, in the record, of the PAVN's 1972 losses in this war against the historical figure, and the rebuilding time that follows from it. Part 2 takes it up under the calendar, because a corps that is weeks late in assembling is a campaign that starts weeks late; if Part 2 does not resolve it, DIRMT01-6 carries it.

**M-2 · The historical outcome satisfies the words of rung (b), and the analysis has kept the two apart by the date alone.** Rung (b) reads: the Republic holds only while American combat forces are present, and their withdrawal means collapse within roughly two years. The last American combat troops left on 29 March 1973; Saigon fell twenty-five months later. Read literally, the historical war is (b). The exercise calls it (a) because rung (a) includes collapse "on the historical timeline". Two memoranda noticed this. Memorandum 002's net assessment says the Republic "did hold while they were present, historically as here, and fell two years after they left, which is the historical outcome and is still called (a) because the date did not" change; memorandum 004's says "the difference between (a) and (b) is not about whether it can fight". Neither drew the consequence. If the only thing separating (a) from (b) is the date, then a change of a single day in the analyst's favour is, on the letter of the ladder, a change of rung. That is a threshold placed exactly on the variable the analysis was meant to estimate freely, and an analyst who does not want to claim a rung move on the strength of a week at Xuan Loc has every reason, conscious or not, never to let the week appear. This audit does not say the analyst yielded to it. It says the structure invited it, and that seven identical dates are what yielding to it would have looked like. **Disposition: remediate** (DIRMT01-1), and a question for the reader of this exercise, who alone may change the ladder: whether (a) is meant to be defined by mechanism (the Republic would have fallen with or without the withdrawal) and (b) by mechanism (it fell because of it), with the date reported beside both. Until the ladder is clarified, DIRMT01-1 requires the date to be reported as its own estimate and not to be read as a rung.

**M-3 · Phase VI's text and Phase VI's end vector disagree, and the vector cannot see anything the fall erased.** Every variable in Phase VI's end vector stands at the historical state. The chapter's own text says otherwise for 1973–74: the Front's district committees in Chuong Thien, Vinh Binh and Kien Hoa lost about a fifth of their members in 1973, about twice the historical rate (EV000-84) [X-006]; the RVNAF was more dependent on American air in 1974 than history's and its President took the Phuoc Long decision "with more conviction" for knowing it (EV000-92) [X-005]; the board's battalions at Duc Duc lost fewer men to desertion than history's (EV000-91) [X-007]. The explanation is structural and not a lapse: Phase VI ends in December 1975, eight months after the Republic ceased to exist, and at that date every variable is historical by construction, because the fall has erased the differences. But that makes the exercise's last measuring point blind to everything except the fall itself. No provision can register in Phase VI's end vector unless it changes the outcome; nothing that changes the *road* to the outcome, including the date, is ever recorded. A war in which Saigon fell on 30 April and a war in which it fell on 15 June 1975 have the same Phase VI end vector. **Disposition: remediate** (DIRMT01-3), with a proposal for the next checkpoint under G2: divide Phase VI at the end of 1974, so that the Republic's state on the eve of the last offensive is measured, and the last campaign is a phase of its own whose dates are an output.

## 2. Unsupported claims

**M-4 · Phase V returns the countryside to history without a sentence about the countryside.** At the end of 1971 the Phase IV chapter holds the countryside at one band better than history on night control and the government's legitimacy, one band worse for the infrastructure, two bands better for finding it, and says that "the VC remain weaker than at any time since 1960 and weaker in the Delta than on the historical course". Twelve months later, at the end of Phase V, all four have returned to the historical state. The Phase V chapter does not contain the words "hamlet" or "pacification" once. The return may be right. The Easter Offensive pulled territorial forces into the main-force war, made almost a million refugees, and set back pacification in Binh Dinh and the northern provinces historically; the historical land programme of 1970 had by 1972 caught up with the land titled here six years early. Each of these is a mechanism by which the gains of 1964–71 could have converged with history. None of them is in the record. The war as written moved four bands in a year by a change of numbers in a chapter's header, which is precisely the "end-state call that moved... without the mechanism being traceable through the chapters" this audit exists to catch. It matters to the reader's question, because the Republic's state in its own countryside in 1973–75 is one of the few things the provisions changed that could bear on how long it lasted. **Disposition: investigate.** What would resolve it: a dated account, in Phase V, of what the offensive did to the held belt of the Delta and the coastal lowlands. Part 2 takes it up under the geography the analysis forgot; DIRMT01-3 forbids the same silent convergence in future.

**M-5 · In 1975, where a provision acted, the outcome was declared and not derived.** The Phase VI chapter contains at least seven places where a provision visibly changes a 1975 event and the next sentence restores the historical outcome by statement:

- At Xuan Loc the 18th Division fights with its squad guns fed "for the first time since January 1974", its marksmen pin the 341st Division's assault battalions at 400 metres, its battalions are board-chosen and its commander was on the 1972 list; and it "held for twelve days, as it held historically", a phrase the event repeats three times, with "I do not adjust these figures" between them (EV000-96).
- "In this war there were more divisions like it: the 22nd's stand on the coast in late March, the Airborne brigades at Phan Rang, and the 25th Division's battalions on Route 1 west of Saigon fought in the same way, and none of them changed the arithmetic of 1975, because the corps above them had already gone." No event shows any of the three; the claim that none changed the arithmetic is the conclusion of an argument not made (EV000-96).
- At Duc Duc the board's battalions held "with fewer men lost to desertion than history's" (EV000-91); in March the 2nd Division loses about 6,000 men to desertion on 28 March exactly as historically (EV000-94), and the chapter does not ask whether the battalions that deserted less in 1974 deserted less in 1975.
- At Phuoc Long the decision not to reinforce is "historical", taken "with more conviction" (EV000-92); a decision taken with more conviction by a President more certain his army cannot hold alone is a different input to 14 March, and the chapter does not follow it.
- The Dalat study proposed a three-month withdrawal; Thieu "took its conclusion and none of its method" (EV000-95). Whether a President who had the study on his desk since January would have issued the Highlands order earlier, later, or differently is not asked; the order is dated 14 March as history dates it.
- Linebacker II returned 21 fewer prisoners, and "the terms are the same" (C005-6); this is reasonable and is stated rather than shown.
- The I Corps and national files are captured (EV000-94, EV000-98). This is the one change that rightly does not touch the date: it changes what happened to people after the fall, not when it came.

None of these is necessarily wrong. A defence that holds for twelve days because the PAVN chose to bypass it on the twelfth day holds for twelve days whatever its ammunition, and that is a real mechanism (the enemy's choice absorbs the difference). But the mechanism has to be stated as such, and in most of these places it is not: the historical outcome is asserted after a paragraph that has just described a different fight. That is the habit the reader's question detected. **Disposition: remediate** (DIRMT01-4).

## 3. Stale positions

**M-6 · POS001-3 holds the timing of Hanoi's decisions fixed, and the record's own history of 1975 shows that timing was the most reactive thing Hanoi did.** POS001-3 says reform raises Hanoi's urgency "and shifts the arguments inside the Politburo; it does not change Hanoi's commitment to armed reunification or the timing of its decisions to invade". The first half is the stronger for seven memoranda of evidence: nothing in the force reaches Hanoi's will (G-14), and the audit reaffirms it. The second half, the timing, was reasonable for 1964–65 and has gone stale. The record's own Phase VI says the North's plan for the last campaign was a two-year campaign to end the war in 1976, adapted after Phuoc Long to strike the Highlands in 1975 (EV000-92), and then, after Ban Me Thuot and the Highlands rout, extended to the Ho Chi Minh Campaign on a schedule the ARVN's collapse dictated. A position that holds the timing of the invasion fixed regardless of the ARVN's behaviour is a position that holds the date of 30 April fixed regardless of anything the provisions do to the ARVN. It is the load-bearing assumption under the whole series' end state, and it was made in the first memorandum about the political reforms of 1964, not about the campaign of 1975. **Disposition: reverse** (REVMT01-1 below). Hanoi's commitment stays fixed. The timing of its offensives, above all the speed of the last one, is a variable every memorandum touching Phases V–VI must test.

**M-7 · The American clock has been judged one memorandum at a time, and never as a sum.** POSCP01-1, made after five memoranda, holds that the small gains on the American clock do not move tolerance by a band in any phase, because the clock was spent by the casualties of 1965–68 and by Tet. Each memorandum since has added its own sub-band saving of American lives and judged it alone: about 1,000–2,500 American lives over the war for the carbines (F004-13) [X-004]; perhaps 1,400–2,700 allied lives in the sieges, most of them American at Con Thien and Khe Sanh (memorandum 005's log) [X-005]; about 1,000–2,000 American lives in 1966–69 for unit rotation (POS006-6) [X-006]; one to three percent of American small-unit deaths in 1965–69 for officer education (POS007-4) [X-007]. Every one of these is an exercise judgment resting on a rate, and DIRCP01-2 rightly forbids treating any of them as a count. But no memorandum has asked what they sum to, and US.casualties stands at the historical band in every phase. Taken together and discounted for overlap, they describe a war in which several thousand fewer Americans died before 1970: a judgment of the order of five to ten percent of the historical American dead, which is below any band but is not nothing, and which nobody has tested against the clock [E: the sum of the four ranges above, less an assumed third for overlap between them]. Separately, the decisive American political event for the date of the fall is not the tolerance of 1968 but the appropriation of 1974 and the refusal of April 1975 (EV000-89, EV000-97), and these have been carried as unaltered history in every memorandum. The record contains things that bear on them in both directions: no Tonkin deception (POS003-3); an honest count of the Trail and the army; the Phoenix scandal of 1967 rather than 1970 (POS006-5); no Bach Mai (EV000-82); and, against them, a police and a national file used by the palace against its opposition from 1966 and consolidated for the 1971 election (POS002-3, POS006-4), which is the material from which the 1973–74 campaign in Congress over the Republic's political prisoners was made. The vote of 1974 has never been examined as a distinct event. **Disposition: investigate,** with DIRMT01-2 for the method. POSCP01-1 stands for 1965–72 until the sum is tested; it is not extended to 1974.

**M-8 · Two lines of the gap register describe a force that no longer exists.** The gap register's latest status was written at CP01, after memorandum 005. G-16 still says "no provision has yet changed how American soldiers are rotated or how advisers are chosen"; memorandum 006 changed both (P006b, P006c). G-4 still says "the divisions' leadership is unchanged because the same few honest officers are spent on every reform at once"; memorandum 007 educated every officer commissioned from 1957 and put board-chosen commanders on every battalion and regiment from 1965–66, while leaving the divisions and corps to the palace (POS007-1, POS007-2). The register is the instrument the next checkpoint and every menu read, and it is wrong in both places. **Disposition: reaffirm with updated reasoning** (the gap status lines below). G-4 is narrowed and not closed: the decisive commands of 1972 and 1975 were still chosen as they were historically, which is the audit's own explanation of why the officer reforms did not move the date. G-16 is narrowed on rotation and advisers and stays open on personnel spaces.

## 4. Metric corruption

**M-9 · The analysis has kept the body count and the Hamlet Evaluation System in their cages; one band rests on thin evidence of a kind the rules invite.** The audit looked for the four failures the procedure names. Body count does not appear as evidence of progress anywhere the audit read. The Hamlet Evaluation System appears in the chapters the audit read once, in Phase III, with the caveat that it was filled in by advisers under pressure to show progress (EV000-54). The exercise's lives-saved figures appear in the Trajectory Log as "perhaps" ranges, and DIRCP01-2 has already disciplined them. No exercise-judgment figure has been found cited later as a measurement. That is a clean result and the audit states it at full weight.

The one place where the method strains is POS007-1, which puts ARVN effectiveness at the top of the band scale, plus three, in 1966–68. The memorandum's own argument is that the gain was invisible to every metric, because the enemy declined to fight the better battalions; the evidence is one ambush not sprung on Route 13 (EV007-3), one Regional Force convoy hit instead, and the COSVN target notes captured with a courier in October 1967 (C007-1). This is not circular, and A13 expressly requires that an invisible improvement be stated. But a band at the cap of the scale on two dated events and a captured document is a large claim on a narrow base, and Phase II was already at plus two before memorandum 007 moved it. **Disposition: investigate.** What would resolve it: two further dated events in Phases II–III, in other corps, showing battalions declined or broken less, before any later memorandum builds on the cap. DIRCP01-2 already states the principle for war-wide figures; DIRMT01-4 extends it.

## 5. Consistency of the enemy

**M-10 · The enemy is brilliant at the regiment and fixed at the Politburo.** Reconstructed from his side, the enemy of these seven memoranda is a coherent campaign in one sense and a strangely divided one in another. Below COSVN he is the most adaptive actor in the record: his regiments decline the better battalion and hit the unreformed one beside it (POS004-1, POS007-1); his district committees leave the held villages within months of the police posts (F002-2); he learns unit rotation's calendar and attacks the relief week (C006-2); he copies the promotion board's lists through an agent in its secretariat (C007-1); he turns the Republic's census into the execution lists of Hue (C002-2); he rebuilds the Trail as a braided net with no point of failure in a single season (POS005-4). Above COSVN, in Hanoi, he has not taken a decision on any date but the historical one in seven memoranda: the Ninth Plenum, the commitment of the regulars, the Tet plan, the third offensive, the Easter Offensive, Resolution 21, the strategy conference of December 1974, Ban Me Thuot, the Ho Chi Minh Campaign. Every one of them is "his own decision, on his own reading" (EV000-87), and every one of them is the historical decision on the historical day.

That is a defensible portrait of the Politburo up to 1973. Its decisions were driven by its own politics, the Sino-Soviet balance and its reading of Washington, none of which the provisions reached (G-14). It is not a defensible portrait of 1975, when Hanoi's schedule was set week by week by what the ARVN did, and when the record's own Phase VI shows the plan changing twice in three months in response to the battlefield. An enemy who adapts within a season to a better rifle but whose timetable for the last campaign is indifferent to whether the ARVN battalions in front of it are better led, better fed with ammunition and less given to desertion, is an enemy given whichever behaviour the end state needed. The audit does not find the opposite failure. The enemy's capability has not been silently degraded where the narrative wanted a success: he has been given full credit for every adaptation, and the analysis has if anything leaned toward his competence. **Disposition: reverse,** through REVMT01-1, so far as the 1975 timetable is concerned. The digest of the enemy's war also shows no event at all in Phase IV in which the enemy held the initiative; G3 requires an unprovoked enemy decision in every memorandum, and the record of 1969–71 has none. That is noted for Part 2's open search.

## 6. Structural bias

**M-11 · Every gain is paid back, in the same way, in the same years.** The analysis has a habit, and the habit has a shape. A provision succeeds where the Republic holds the night; its success pushes it into ground where it cannot succeed, or produces a record the enemy captures, or creates an institution the palace captures when the American lever lapses; and the memorandum closes on the irony. The census becomes the Hue execution lists (C002-2). The old rifles handed down arm the Front (C004-1, C004-4). The hoards built against the ration are left for the PAVN (C004-5). The audited Phoenix file is captured whole because its quality made it worth consolidating (C006-3). The promotion board's lists are copied by the enemy, and its proof of success is the record of its betrayal (C007-1). The best staff plan becomes the warrant for the worst decision (C007-5). Each of these is plausible, and several are well grounded in what the historical record shows of the same institutions. Taken together they are a template, and CP01 already noticed its first sign when it found every chain resolving exactly as predicted (DIRCP01-1).

The template also has a calendar. The Commission was subordinated in July 1971 (C001-5); the Selection Board captured by the Prime Minister as the lever lapsed (POS002-2); the general-officer list made advisory on 12 January 1970 (POS007-2); the Phung Hoang file moved into the CIO in 1970 (POS006-4). Four institutions, built by four memoranda, all fall to the same mechanism in the same eighteen months, and each memorandum judged its own institution's fall alone. No memorandum asked whether four honest institutions that had guarded each other for six years (the land programme gave the Commission its constituency, the Commission audited Phoenix, the Board's chiefs ran the police) were harder for one President to capture than one, or easier, because they gave him four reasons to fear the same constituency. The answer may be "no different". It has not been given.

The bias the audit does *not* find is the one the procedure names first. The analysis has not favoured ISR over firepower or the main-force war over the village war; if anything it has given the village war more depth and more credit than the main-force war. But it has favoured one kind of ending, the gain that is undone, and that preference is what makes "nothing changed by a day" feel like a conclusion when it is partly a style. **Disposition: remediate** (DIRMT01-5).

## 7. The unfought fights

**M-12 · The date.** This is the finding the reader's question was really about, and it gathers M-1, M-2, M-3, M-5, M-6 and M-10.

The analysis has never fought for the date. It has fought, honestly and at length, for the rung, and its argument for the rung is the strongest thing in the record. Taking the reader's list in turn:

- *Land reform and an honest government* (P001a, P001b, P002b) changed what the government's arrival meant in the held belt, and the analysis credited it at full weight as the first provision to touch the deficit the assessment ranked first. Its institutions were captured when the American lever lapsed in 1970–71, and the Republic that met 1975 was "more stable than at any time since 1963 but narrowly based".
- *The war on the infrastructure* (P002a, P006a) ground the infrastructure out of the held belt three years before the historical Phoenix reached that layer, and it addressed the half of the find-and-fix problem that the last campaign did not turn on: the Republic was not destroyed by its infrastructure in 1975 but by fourteen to fifteen PAVN divisions in the open.
- *Precision air strikes and persistent reconnaissance* (P003a, P003b, P005a–d) were American, operated by Americans, and went home with them in 1973; Case–Church wrote that into law, and the Republic was left with plans built around them.
- *Advisory reform* (P006c) deepened influence, not authority, and left in March 1973.
- *Partly reformed officers* (P007a–c) reformed the battalions and regiments and stopped at the divisions and corps, where the President's choices commanded in 1972 and 1975.

Every item on the list either left, was captured, or stopped below the level at which the end was decided. That is a real pattern, not a stylistic one, and it is why the audit reaffirms rung (a). It is also a statement about the provisions the exercise has received: none of the eighteen gives the Republic an air arm it owns, ammunition and fuel stocks that survive the 1974 appropriation, or a lever on the choice of its corps commanders that outlives the American one, and the gap register and CP01's menu have said for two memoranda that those are what the end turns on.

But the rung and the date are different questions, and the record does not support the claim that the date did not move. On the evidence already in the chapters, the following mechanisms pull the fall **earlier**:

- The ARVN's defence of every besieged town was built for a decade around the B-52J and the coordinate cell, and its officers and President knew in 1974 precisely what they lacked (F005-20, EV000-91, EV000-92). The Phuoc Long decision was taken "with more conviction", and the abandonment of the Highlands followed from a President who had already concluded that his army could not hold a besieged town alone (C005-7).
- The ration of 1974 fell harder on an army built for ten years around the squad gun (F004-15, EV000-89), and the battalions that "avoided contacts they could not break with volume" in 1974 were the ones that met the offensive.
- Nine million rounds of linked 5.56mm were held in the I and II Corps hoards and lost with them (C004-5).

And these pull it **later**:

- The PAVN's 1972 losses at the measured sieges should have been higher than history's (M-1), and with them the time the North needed to rebuild its corps.
- Board-chosen battalions held their companies together with fewer desertions at Duc Duc (EV000-91), and "more divisions like" the 18th fought the last campaign (EV000-96): the 22nd Division on the coast, the Airborne brigades at Phan Rang, the 25th Division west of Saigon.
- The 18th Division at Xuan Loc fought with its squad guns fed and its marksmen killing the assault battalions at 400 metres (EV000-96).
- The Delta's Front was weaker than history's from 1965 and lost district committees at twice the historical rate in 1973 (EV000-84); the IV Corps that was still intact on 30 April historically was more intact here.
- The Dalat study of November 1974 existed and was on the President's desk from January (EV000-95).

The net direction of these two lists is not obvious, and this audit does not pretend to settle it. There are good reasons the pulls may cancel: the PAVN chose its battles in 1975 as in every other year, and a stronger division in its path was bypassed rather than fought, as Xuan Loc was; the collapse in Saigon on 28–30 April was a political collapse after encirclement, and a better-led 25th Division does not change when General Minh surrenders. There is also a reason the date may not be a smooth quantity at all. Hanoi's plan, as the baseline records it, aimed at 1976 and was accelerated twice in three months. A delay that the PAVN could absorb within the dry season would change the date by days; a delay that pushed the final battle past the rains would change it by most of a year. Whether such a delay was within reach of the cumulative force is the single most important question the exercise has not asked. It requires the calendar of 1975, and Part 2's search heading on the calendar as strategy is where it belongs.

What the audit can say firmly is that "not by a day" is the product of a measuring instrument that could not record a day (M-3), a ladder that would have counted a day as a rung (M-2), a standing position that held Hanoi's timing fixed (M-6), and a habit of restoring historical outcomes by statement in the chapter that describes the end (M-5). Each of those is a defect of method. Together they are sufficient to explain the seven identical dates without any of the analysis's judgments about the war being wrong. That is the finding: the date is not wrong, it is untested.

**Disposition: investigate,** in Part 2 of this meta-checkpoint, under the calendar and the geography of 1975; and **remediate** for every later memorandum (DIRMT01-1, DIRMT01-6). The end state is not changed by this Part: the chapters still record 30 April 1975, and under E4 the chapters win until they are changed. Part 2 changes them if the calendar it writes gives a different date, and changes the end-state file with them; if it does not, it says why at the weight this finding asks.
