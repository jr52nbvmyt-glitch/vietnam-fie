## 8. Revised trajectory

### The re-judgments: night control by corps

This answers the user's second question: does the VCI campaign make a big difference to the rate of securing the countryside? In the units the question deserves, the answer is that it makes a large difference in two corps, a small one in the other two, and that the two small ones decide the war. The facets below are judged for the first time in the record. The parent names II Corps as its binding facet in every phase to 1974, because the Highlands and the coast decided in 1972 and 1975 whether the held belt could be reached at all.

The pattern has a mechanism behind each part of it:
- **III and IV Corps, 1966–68.** The gain is largest here. The region's collections from the posted villages fell to an eighth of 1964's (EV012-4). Hoc Mon had no resident cadres by Tet (EV012-8). About 900,000 hectares were titled. The posts had radios and were attacked a third less often (EV008-4). In III Corps this means roughly one rural family in ten that slept under the Front's authority in history's war slept under the government's in this one.
- **The gain shrinks in 1969–71.** This is the HES's ceiling, not the war. History's own count reached 95% A–C by November 1970, so a held belt held more firmly shows up on the ground and not in the count (A13).
- **It returns in 1972–74.** The Easter Offensive took back the gains of 1964–71 only where a PAVN division came into the peopled lowlands (EVMT01-1). The Delta's posts held (EVMT01-2), and the Front's district committees lost twice the historical share of their members in 1973 (EV000-84).
- **I and II Corps never move more than a few points.** The task force knew the coast thinly, and Hoa Thanh's story worked against it in Phu Yen (EV012-5). The Highlands were a main-force war that no file reached.

**The answer to the user's question, then.** The VCI campaign, with the land, the police and the posts beside it, secured the countryside around Saigon and in the Delta about six months to a year ahead of history, by these figures: the level this war reached by the end of 1967 is about the level history reached during 1968, after Tet and at the start of the Accelerated Pacification Campaign. It did not secure the two corps in which the war was lost. That is why the end state moves only as far as a Republic of III and IV Corps.

### The trajectory, phase by phase

**Phase I (1964–Jul 1965).** The held belt begins to be held, about 3 to 8 points above history in III and IV Corps by July 1965. HAMMER FALL takes the North's economy in a week and moves nothing in the South (POSCP03-1). Nothing further on the record moves this phase.

**Phase II (Aug 1965–1967).** This is the force's strongest phase. The Front's government of the Saigon belt is broken from its edge, the posts hold the held belt, and the ARVN battalion is better below the regiment. The main-force war is history's. The enemy initiates about two engagements in three, as he did historically.

**Phase III (1968).** Tet keeps its turn. It is cheaper in the Delta, warned only at corps level (POSCP03-2), and the countryside is taken faster after it.

**Phase IV (1969–71).** This is the inheritance and its capture, as CP02 judged. The held belt's gain hides under the HES's ceiling.

**Phase V (1972).** The gains are lost where the PAVN came and kept where it did not.

**Phase VI (1973–75).** The reprieve of a year is held by memorandum 011's removal of the break-in. Thieu's refusal of 11–14 March and the Politburo's decision to wait for the dry season follow from it. The held belt makes the reprieve worth having: a Republic of III and IV Corps with 60–85% of their rural people held after dark.

**The A9 call stays at rung (b), dependent survival.** The Republic stands past 30 April 1975 and is expected to fall between February and May 1976. The rung test of memorandum 013 stands unchanged, because nothing in this checkpoint changes Phase VI's events.

**Why rung (c) is not reached.** It needs a Republic that survives a year without American air power (c2, c3). The PAVN's five corps, the 130mm gun and the ARVN's divisions and corps are unchanged by any provision, and the record holds nothing that would change them.

**The date test (DIRMT01-1).** The window stays February to May 1976. Nothing in this checkpoint moves a mechanism that sets it. The Tet finding and the calibration of memorandum 013 both concern 1965–68.

## 9. Limiting factors

Ranked by how directly each blocks rung (c):

1. **Who commands the corps and divisions** (G-4). This is unchanged since CP02. The spring of 1975 was decided by corps commanders' orders and by the PAVN's choice of ground. Thirteen memoranda have improved every command below the regiment and none above it.
2. **The Republic's own fire against the 130mm and the T-54** (ARVN.self_sufficiency.fire_support). By the end of 1974 the Republic owns about 3 to 5 points more of its own fire support than history's Republic, and nothing outranges the PAVN's guns. This is the binding facet in Phase VI.
3. **II Corps** (POP.night_control.II_corps). The held belt never reached the Highlands or the central coast. Ban Me Thuot falls in 1975 to an offensive that the provisions' best work could not see or slow.
4. **The soldier's family** (G-17). The corps dissolve toward their families in March 1975 in this war as in history's. That is so even though the retreat is three to five weeks later.
5. **American money after 1974** (US.political_tolerance.congress). About $950 million to $1,275 million for fiscal 1975 against history's $700 million buys a year and no more. Congress will not fly the bombers.
6. **Finding the main force under canopy** (G-7). No provision has given the allies the enemy's divisions before they moved.

## 11. The bill

1. Corps and division commanders the Republic chose on merit.
2. Fire the Republic owns that outranges the PAVN's 130mm and stops its armour.
3. A way into II Corps.
4. A soldier whose family is safe when his unit fights.
5. The PAVN's divisions found before they move.
6. Money beyond 1975, which no military provision can buy.
