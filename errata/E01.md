# E01 — Memorandum 003: the RQ-4B and U-2S work with GPS and satellite links, under the standing condition of 23 Nov 2026

wrong: "In January 1964 there is no GPS, and there will be none for fourteen years; there are no military communications satellites in orbit [H]. … My reading, therefore, is that each RQ-4 is delivered with what it minimally needs to take off, navigate and land without satellites — a ground-based local positioning array at its home runway to stand in for differential GPS, and an inertial navigation solution corrected in flight against radar imagery of surveyed ground — and that its control and its data travel only by line-of-sight link"; card P003b: "Links: line of sight only, about 200–250 nm from a terminal" and "Navigation: inertial with radar map-matching and a local positioning array at the home runway"; negative specification: "within about 250 nm of Bien Hoa or Da Nang"; P003a D6: "A 2026 U-2 in the Middle East would also pass data beyond line of sight by satellite; in 1964 it cannot, because no satellite exists to carry it [H]", with northern collection "hours after the event, not minutes".
correct: By standing condition, the GPS and military SATCOM constellations are present from the campaign's opening [U: standing condition, set 23 Nov 2026; recorded in rules/CONFIG.toml [conditions]]. Both provisions therefore arrive and fly exactly as they fly in 2026, and the Part 1 reading is withdrawn. (1) P003b navigates by GPS-aided inertial navigation and takes off and lands on differential GPS corrections from its own Launch and Recovery Element [M003·S7]; there is no site-specific positioning array, and the "array damaged by a mortar round" failure mode does not exist. Any runway its LRE kit is set up on will serve. (2) P003b is controlled and passes its data both by line of sight and by satellite [M003·S6] [M003·S7]. Its reach is set by its endurance and the threat, not by a ground terminal: it can hold an orbit over the Red River delta, the Gulf of Tonkin, the whole of the Laotian panhandle and Cambodia, and the Mission Control Element need not sit beside the runway. A lost line-of-sight link falls back to satellite control. The Da Nang relay terminal is not needed for control; Da Nang remains a recovery alternate and a direct line-of-sight data receiver. (3) P003a passes SIGINT, radar and electro-optical data beyond line of sight by Ku-band satellite through the dorsal-pod satellite datalinks (Senior Span, Senior Spur) when out of tether range [S13], so northern collection reaches the ground station in near real time, not after landing [S14]. (4) Nothing else in Part 1 changes: the sensors, altitudes, endurance, canopy blindness, the SA-2 threat, personnel spaces and the find-and-fix limits stand as written. (5) The condition gives the constellations, not user equipment: the historical 1964–75 force has no GPS receivers or satellite terminals (A3a). Coordinates these aircraft generate are GPS-accurate; the aircraft, artillery and ground units that must use them navigate and bomb by the period's methods (map grids, LORAN, TACAN, radar bombing).
