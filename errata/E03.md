correct: The grant includes the Regional and Popular Forces [U: the user's ruling of 24 Nov 2026 in the memorandum 010 thread, "No, RF/PF is included"]. P010b covers every NCO of the RVNAF regular forces and of the Regional and Popular Forces (the Civil Guard and Self-Defense Corps before 1964) from 1957, on the same sequence of courses and the same gates. (1) Courses: the territorial NCO takes the same Basic, Advanced, Senior and Master Leader Courses as the regular NCO [U]; the Basic Leader Course is taught to RF and PF corporals and squad leaders at the RF/PF training centres (37 cut to 19 under the Central Training Command per M009·S12) with an RF/PF wing at the Nha Trang academy for the senior courses [E: the regular forces' arrangement applied to the territorial school system]. The PF platoon leader, a sergeant or sergeant first class leading a platoon of about 35 men [M009·S11], holds the Advanced Leader Course before he takes the platoon. (2) Quantity: RF and PF NCOs number about 10,000–15,000 in 1957, about 25,000–40,000 on 1 Jan 1964 and about 55,000–80,000 at the 1969 peak [E: an NCO share of 12–20% of a Civil Guard and Self-Defense Corps of about 86,000 in 1959 per M009·S10 and of 391,000 RF and PF in 1969 per M009·S11, at four to seven NCOs in a 35-man PF platoon and 15–20 in a 123-man RF company per M009·S10]. The territorial forces add about 6,000–10,000 course completions a year in 1957–63, 12,000–20,000 in 1964–67 and 20,000–35,000 at the 1968–70 peak [E: same basis]. The combined P010b throughput is therefore about 14,000–24,000 a year in 1957–63, 27,000–45,000 in 1964–67 and 50,000–80,000 at the peak. (3) Cost: about 500–1,000 more Vietnamese instructors before 1964 and 1,500–2,500 more at the peak, drawn from RF and regular NCOs [E: the 2026 ratio of one to 12–16]; about 60–120 more US personnel in RVN, of whom 50–110 military spaces, for advisory teams at the RF/PF centres [E: two to five advisers at each of 19–37 centres]. P010b's US cost becomes about 210–420 personnel, of whom about 180–380 military spaces; no troop ceiling is set (CONFIG). (4) Reach and preconditions: the territorial NCO's school is near his province, but his release depends on the province and district chiefs who controlled RF/PF manning [M009]; the gate gives them the same incentive it gives the regular commander. The territorial NCO's family lives in or near the hamlet he guards (DIRMT01-9), so his absence at a course leaves the post a leader short and his family where the enemy can reach it. (5) Effect: the gap the course closes is widest here. Historically, PF leadership training ran up to 60% behind programme in 1964 and its quotas were never filled [M010·S9]. Under the grant every PF squad and platoon leader holds the course for his grade, and the PF platoon in the hamlet at night is led by a sergeant who has been taught to site its position, post its listening posts, control its fire and hold it together until the reaction force arrives. P010b pushes RFPF.effectiveness +1 in addition to its regular-force pushes [X-010]. (6) Withdrawn: the negative-specification bullets for P010b and P010a that say the provision does not reach the Regional and Popular Forces, and the clause of the P010b verdict "It does not reach the territorial forces". Everything else in Part 1 stands, including the double drain of D2, which this widens: the RF/PF centres' instructors come from the same thin layer of senior territorial NCOs.
