# Memorandum 011 — Part 2 of 3 · §2 Integration and second-order effects · §3 Changes to existing forces


## §2 Part A — Integration

### Functional systems

All three provisions join one functional system, and only one: SUSTAIN, in its widest sense. SUSTAIN here is the American capacity to keep paying for this war in money, men and patience. None of them finds, fixes, strikes or interdicts anything. None of them holds a hamlet. They act on the clock, and the clock is the system on which every other one depends, because every provision in the index arrives, operates and leaves on a timetable Washington sets. I have written for ten memoranda that the clock was spent by the casualties of 1965–68 and by Tet, and that no provision had touched it. This one touches it. The question for this part is where, and by how much.

The three provisions act on three parts of American tolerance, at three dates, and they barely overlap. The volunteers act on the public, through the draft, from 1965 to 1972, with their weight in 1966–69. The removal of My Lai acts on the public's knowledge of the war and on the credibility gap from November 1969, with its weight in 1969–71. The removal of Watergate acts on the administration and on Congress from May 1973 to the end, with its weight in the summer of 1974 and the spring of 1975. The first two act while the Americans are fighting. The third acts after they have gone, on what is left of the American commitment once it is written in statutes rather than troop orders.

**F011-1** **The package acts on the clock in two halves that do not meet.** The volunteers and the removal of My Lai act on the public while American troops are in the field. On the record, the public's verdict on the war had already turned by August 1968, when 53% called it a mistake¹, and the decision of 31 March 1968 that set the war on the road to withdrawal was taken before either provision's main effect. The removal of Watergate acts on the administration and Congress after March 1973, when the troops have gone and the question is no longer how long Americans will fight but how much they will pay and whether they will return. The two halves do not compound, because the first half's gain is spent by 1971 and the second half's begins in 1973. Read together, they are a small gain on the clock of the American war and a larger one on the clock of the Republic's war after it.

The provisions make one system newly viable that the force has never had: a credible American commitment to the Republic after March 1973. That commitment existed historically on paper, in the President's letters of November 1972 and January 1973¹. Under the removal of Watergate it exists in the person of a President who can still argue for it. It still does not exist in law: the Case–Church amendment, which I judge passes in substantially its historical form in 60–75% of cases², forbids American combat in Indochina from 15 August 1973 without a new vote of Congress¹.

### Compound pairings
**Interactions with earlier provisions**

- P011a × P008a, P008b: a larger fiscal 1975 appropriation (about $850 million – $1.1 billion against $700 million,²) is spent through the Bien Hoa arsenal and the battery works, which stretched each ammunition and communications dollar by about one and a half and two times; the stretch is applied to more dollars, and the margin goes to fuel and aircraft parts, which the base could not stretch
- P011a × P005a, P005b, P005c: the enforcement threat's instrument is the B-52J with GPS weapons, at Guam and U-Tapao on the alert pads; a President with his authority intact can ask Congress to let it fly, but the coordinate cell that fed it was disbanded in September 1973 and nothing in the VNAF can pass it a target
- P011a × P007b: the Dalat study of November 1974, "The Defence of the Republic on Reduced Resources", is written for a smaller ration than the one the Republic receives here, and a President who believes the bombers may return is less likely to read it
- P011b × P010a × P006b: in the rifle companies of 1969–71, the volunteer who chose to come, the sergeant schooled for his grade and the battalion that trained and deployed together; the indiscipline of the drawdown falls further in the units that had all three
- P011c × P007a × P010a: the educated lieutenant and the schooled sergeant act on the command climate below the company; the removal of My Lai removes the Peers inquiry that acted on it from above, and the two do not substitute for each other
- P011c × P006a, P002b: the audited Phoenix count and the Inspectorate's province reports narrowed the credibility gap by giving MACV honest numbers; the removal of My Lai narrows it by removing the starkest proof of a dishonest report, and the two together make MACV's word in 1970–71 worth more than history's

**F011-2** **The larger appropriation and the Republic's own arsenal multiply, and the product reaches fuel and aircraft for the first time.** On the historical course the fiscal 1975 appropriation was about $700 million, and the Bien Hoa arsenal and the Go Vap battery works stretched it to buy what about $850 million – $1 billion had bought historically, in ammunition and radios but not in fuel, aircraft or anything imported whole. Under the removal of Watergate the appropriation itself is about $850 million – $1.1 billion. The stretch applies to the part spent on propellant, explosive and components, and the rest buys fuel, aircraft parts and the 155mm and 175mm rounds Bien Hoa could not forge. I put the product at what about $1.0 – 1.4 billion would have bought historically, against fiscal 1974's $1.1 billion², with the upper bound marked because it multiplies two judgments that both lean the same way. That is roughly the fiscal 1974 level held through fiscal 1975 instead of cut by a third. It is not abundance. It is the difference between the 1974 ration and a war fought on the 1973 allowance, and its first visible effect is the VNAF's aircraft and helicopters, which history grounded for want of fuel and parts and which the stretch never reached³.

**F011-3** **The bombers are on the pads, and the threat that would send them back has no eyes.** The enforcement instrument the President's letters promised is, in this war, the B-52J with GBU-31 and GBU-38 bombs, sitting on Strategic Air Command alert from August 1973. A President with his authority intact can make the case to Congress for sending it, and I judge that in some cases he would. But the coordinate cell that turned American reconnaissance into GPS aimpoints was disbanded in September 1973, and its folders went to Clark and the Defense Mapping Agency. The VNAF has no aircraft that can carry a guided bomb or pass a coordinate. If Congress let the bombers fly in March 1975, they would arrive over the Highlands with no one on the ground to tell them where the 10th and 316th Divisions were, and the precision that made the J a transformation against fixed targets would be worth little against corps on the move⁴. The threat is a political instrument before it is a military one, and its value lies almost entirely in what Hanoi believes about it.

### The find-to-fix chain against the main force

No effect, and I say why. None of the three provisions adds a sensor, a reconnaissance unit or a means of holding an enemy who chooses whether to fight. The volunteers serve in the same rifle companies as the draftees they replaced, on the same patrols. The enforcement threat of 1973–75, if it worked at all, would work by deterring the main force's offensives, not by finding it. The finding problem of 1973–75 is the Defense Attaché Office's and the Joint General Staff's, and it stands as the chapters leave it: the PAVN was seen massing in the Highlands in early 1975, and Ban Me Thuot was still a surprise.

### The identify-to-neutralise chain against the VCI

Almost no effect. The removal of My Lai takes away the Front's best single story in Quang Ngai, a province it had held for a generation, and that story was told in every hamlet of Son Tinh District for years. Its absence does not make a single villager name a cadre to the Special Branch. The infrastructure in Quang Ngai was identified, where it was identified at all, by the district centres and the police files of memoranda 002 and 006⁵, which the removal neither strengthens nor weakens. The other two provisions do not reach the countryside.

### Dependence on SECURE

The package's effect in the countryside is a trace, and it depends on SECURE only at one point. Son My's people live. Whether the Republic gains anything from that depends entirely on who holds Son My after dark, and in 1968–71 that was the Front, with the 48th Local Force Battalion in the district. The village that history emptied stays full of people under the Front's administration. Its young men are available to the Front's recruiters, its rice to the Front's tax collectors. I count that honestly: the removal of a massacre committed by the Republic's ally does not make the village the Republic's. It removes a grievance from a village the government did not hold, and that grievance was the Front's most valuable political asset in the district.

The removal of Watergate depends on SECURE in 1973–75 through the appropriation. A larger fiscal 1975 appropriation keeps the territorial posts' radios on the air and their ammunition points stocked, which memorandum 008 had already done in part³, and adds fuel for the relief columns and helicopters that the posts' defence depended on. The territorial war of 1974, where history's district chiefs found their posts going silent and their reaction forces without fuel, is the part of the Republic's war where a dollar does most.

### The ADVISE cluster

The package feeds the advisory cluster in one way that matters, and it is after the advisers have gone. From March 1973 the American lever over the Republic is no longer a field adviser or the Aid Compliance Board, which lapsed with the drawdown. It is the aid itself and the President's word behind it. A President with his authority intact holds that lever more firmly than history's, and he could have used it: to make the Republic shorten its line, rebuild a reserve, move the soldiers' families, or adopt the Dalat staff college's plan of November 1974 for a defence on reduced resources⁶. Nothing in the record says Nixon would have done any of that, and his letters argue the other way. They promised Thieu support for holding everything, not advice on what to give up¹. Advisory leverage after 1973 was always going to be spent on survival rather than reform, and the removal of Watergate gives the President more of it to spend. I judge it more likely to be spent reassuring Saigon than reshaping it.

The volunteers and the removal of My Lai do not touch the advisory cluster. The advisers of memorandum 006 were already volunteers, chosen by board⁵.

**F011-4** **The President's restored authority is a lever on Saigon he is unlikely to pull.** From mid-1973 the only American leverage over Thieu is the aid and the promise, and both are stronger under this provision. A President who used them to make the Republic choose, between holding everything on a thin ration and holding the populated country with a reserve, would be using the one instrument that could have reached the decisions of March 1975. I judge that he does not use it that way. The promise was made to get Thieu to sign in January 1973, its whole content was that the Republic need not give anything up, and a President who means to keep it has no reason to tell Thieu to plan for its failure. This is a moderate finding, and it points in both directions. The lever exists, and it stays in the drawer.

### Employment concepts

#### EC011-1 · The letters kept: an enforcement threat with a President behind it

**What it is.** From the Paris Agreement to the end of the second term, the administration keeps the President's promise of November 1972¹ alive as declared policy: in public statements on violations, in the President's own messages to Hanoi through the channels Kissinger used in Paris, and in visible readiness. That means the B-52Js held on alert at Andersen and U-Tapao and carrier groups of the Seventh Fleet off the coast in the dry season, when the North's offensives came⁷.

**Profile.** The cycle is the dry season. From October to May each year the administration watches for a major violation and repeats the warning. It does not bomb, because from 15 August 1973 bombing needs Congress¹. A response requires the President to ask Congress for authority, and Congress to vote within days. The minimum viable package is the President's request, a majority in both houses, and the bombers ready. Without the second, the concept degrades to a bluff, and its value then depends on how long Hanoi takes to call it.

**Dependence.** It depends on Case–Church being weaker than history's (P011a, a 25–40% case,²) or on a Congress willing to vote authority in a crisis. In the more likely case, where Case–Church stands, it degrades to what EC011-3 found impossible: a threat that cannot lawfully be carried out.

#### EC011-2 · The early supplemental: asking before the collapse

**What it is.** A President not fighting impeachment asks Congress for the Republic's money in the spring of 1974, for fiscal 1975, with the full weight of his office. In the summer of 1974 history's President was resigning, and history's request was made by an unelected successor in April 1975, after the collapse had begun¹. Here the request comes earlier, from Nixon to the Congress elected in 1972, and Murray's warning that cuts could be equated with loss of territory is the President's argument, not the attaché's.

**Profile.** One request a year, in the spring, for the fiscal year starting 1 July. The concept's cycle is the appropriation calendar: hearings in the spring, House and Senate votes in the summer, conference in the autumn. Its package is the President's personal advocacy and the votes of perhaps 15–30 Republicans in the House who historically deserted the administration in 1973–74 and here do not⁸.

**Result.** The fiscal 1975 appropriation is the concept's yield, and I judge it at about $850 million – $1.1 billion. In April 1975 a second request, an emergency supplemental like Ford's for $722 million¹, goes to a Congress with 15–30 more Republicans than history's². I judge that request still more likely to fail than pass, because by April the Republic is collapsing and Congress will not pay to rebuild an army that has just abandoned a third of the country.

#### EC011-3 · Bombing in spite of the law (abandoned)

**What was tried.** In planning, not in action. After Phuoc Long fell on 6 January 1975, the President's staff studied whether he could order the bombers back on his own authority, as Commander-in-Chief responding to a violation of an agreement the United States had signed, despite the 15 August prohibition.

**Why it was abandoned.** Because it was illegal, and a President who had survived his second term without scandal would not stake it on breaking a statute passed by more than two-thirds of both houses¹. It would also have failed on its own terms. The Js would have gone over a province already lost, with no coordinate cell to aim them, against a 4th Corps that had already left the town to its security troops. The abandoned concept names the constraint the dossier had to leave open: the provision restores a President, not his power to make war.

#### EC011-4 · The volunteer in the line

**What it is.** The way the Army uses the about 464,000 men who enlisted instead of waiting to be drafted: exactly as it used them as draftees, in the same jobs and units⁹. In practice about 162,000 of them serve in South Vietnam¹, most in the infantry, in the rifle companies of the 1st, 4th, 9th and 25th Infantry Divisions, the 1st Cavalry Division, the 101st Airborne Division, the 23rd (Americal) Infantry Division and the separate brigades¹⁰.

**Profile.** A twelve-month tour, like every man's. The cycle that matters is the replacement or unit-rotation cycle of memorandum 006: the volunteer arrives with his unit or as a replacement, serves his year, and goes home⁵. In a rifle company of about 140–160 men in 1969, about 40–60 were draftees on history's course¹¹. Here about 10–15 of them are volunteers.

**What it changes on a Tuesday.** Little that a patrol leader would see. The volunteer does not shoot better or walk more quietly. He is somewhat less likely to be the man who refuses to go out, uses heroin in the bunker line or threatens his sergeant. In 1969–71 that is not nothing.

#### EC011-5 · Volunteers for the combat arms by contract (abandoned)

**What was tried.** The Army's recruiting command, given about 30–60,000 more volunteers a year, proposed in 1966 to sign them on contracts that guaranteed their choice of combat arm and their division, as the 2026 Army does for its own volunteers, and to market the infantry to them.

**Why it was abandoned.** Because the ruling puts them in the jobs the draft would have given them⁹, and because a guaranteed choice of unit would have made the Army's assignment of infantry replacements in 1966–68 unworkable. The constraint it names is that the Army needed infantrymen where the casualties fell, not where volunteers chose to go. The concept was replaced by EC011-4.

#### EC011-6 · Son Tinh as an ordinary district

**What it is.** How the war is fought in Son Tinh District from 16 March 1968 without the massacre. Task Force Barker's companies search the Son My hamlets on 16 March, find no main-force unit, and leave¹. The Americal Division's operations in Quang Ngai through 1968–69 continue as history records them: the search-and-destroy sweeps, the free-fire areas on the Batangan peninsula, the harassment and interdiction fire, and the refugee camps on Route 1.

**Profile.** The cycle is the task force's: three to five days in the field, two to three days at Landing Zone Dottie, repeated, with the 48th Local Force Battalion slipping out of each sweep. The province's population still lives under a war fought among it. The difference is one village, and the absence of the story the Front would have told about it in every other village.

#### EC011-7 · The command-climate inquiry without a scandal (abandoned)

**What was tried.** In 1970, without My Lai and the Peers inquiry to force it, MACV's Inspector General proposed a theater-wide inquiry into how body-count reporting shaped the conduct of operations in the Americal Division and elsewhere. It drew on the reports of the 23rd Division's own inspector general on drug and discipline cases¹² and on the audited Phoenix counts⁵.

**Why it was abandoned.** Because no one above MACV wanted it, and MACV could not do it alone. Without a massacre in the newspapers, an inquiry into the command climate of a division in the middle of the drawdown was a confession with no audience that demanded it, and it would have been read in Washington as an attack on the withdrawal's management. The concept names the constraint that the counter-directional effect of the removal turns on: in this army, reckoning came only when a scandal forced it.

## §2 Part B — Channels

### Displacement

**F011-5** **The case against the war loses two of its emblems and finds others.** The antiwar movement does not depend on the draft or on My Lai. It depends on a war that has gone on four years without a visible end, at a cost of about 300 American dead a week in 1968. Remove a quarter of the draft notices, and the movement's organisers turn to the casualty lists and the cost. Remove My Lai, and the press and the movement use other evidence of what the war did to the villages: the free-fire zones, the refugee camps, the Pentagon Papers of June 1971, and the veterans' testimony that came home with the men. The displacement is real and incomplete. My Lai was the most powerful single image of the war's methods because of its scale and its cover-up, and no other incident combined both. The movement's moral argument is weaker for losing it, perhaps noticeably. Its political argument is untouched.

**F011-6** **Hanoi's reading of America moves from the White House to the statute book.** History's North read the American political collapse of 1973–74 as its signal. Under the removal of Watergate it cannot read collapse, because there is none. The Politburo's analysts shift their attention to what remains: the Case–Church amendment, the War Powers Resolution, and the aid vote. Those were already the real constraints, and the North's analysts were good enough to see it. The test of January 1975 at Phuoc Long asks the question that matters, which is not whether the President wants to respond but whether the law will let him. I judge that Hanoi still asks it, perhaps on the same date. The 4th Corps' attack was set by the dry season and the B-2 Front's readiness, not only by the Watergate calendar¹³.

### Bottleneck migration

**F011-7** **The binding constraint on American support moves from the President to Congress, and in Saigon from the money to the decisions of March 1975.** Before the provision, the binding constraint on the American commitment after 1973 had three parts: a President who had lost his authority, a Congress that had written its prohibitions into law, and an appropriation cut by a third. The removal of Watergate lifts the first part and loosens the third. The second becomes the whole constraint: whether Congress will let the bombers fly and pay for the Republic's war. In Saigon, the binding constraint on survival moves away from the ration and toward the decisions the chapters already identify as the critical path: Thieu's orders of 13 and 14 March, the lack of a reserve, and the soldiers' families on the road. The more the provision gives the Republic in money, the more plainly the war turns on those decisions, and none of them is about money.

### Own-force opportunity cost

**F011-8** **The President's capital and the recruiters' time are the only scarce things the package consumes, and the first is the one that matters.** The volunteers cost the Army's recruiting command perhaps 5–10% more recruiters in the peak years¹, a cost that falls in the United States and spends no space in theater. The removal of My Lai consumes nothing. The removal of Watergate gives the President about two years of political capital that history's President spent defending himself. What he spends it on is the opportunity cost. Détente with Moscow, the opening to China, the oil embargo of October 1973 and the recession of 1974 all compete for it. Every hour of the President's time and every favour in Congress spent on the Republic's appropriation is not spent on the trade bill or the energy crisis. I judge that a President in that position spends enough on the Republic to raise its appropriation and not enough to change the law.

### Counterfactual erasure

**F011-9** **The removal of Watergate erases the events of 1973–74 that made the American withdrawal from Indochina irreversible in law.** What no longer happens: the Senate Watergate Committee's hearings from 17 May 1973, the Saturday Night Massacre of 20 October 1973, the articles of impeachment of July 1974, the resignation of 9 August 1974 and the pardon of 8 September¹. Ford does not become President, and the 1974 midterm swing is smaller. What becomes of the attention they consumed is the important part. History's Congress spent 1973–74 in a confrontation with a President it was removing, and every vote on Indochina was also a vote in that confrontation. The War Powers override of 7 November 1973, eighteen days after the Saturday Night Massacre, is the plainest example. Without the confrontation, some of those votes are cast on the war's own merits, and on its merits Congress still wanted to be done with Indochina. I judge the War Powers override about even and the 1974 aid vote moderately larger, not the law of 1973 different in substance.

**F011-10** **The removal of My Lai erases the Peers inquiry, the Calley trial and the national argument of 1971, and the attention moves to the Pentagon Papers.** What no longer happens: the Hersh story of 13 November 1969 in the week of the Mobilization, the Haeberle photographs of 20 November, the Peers inquiry from 24 November 1969 and its report of March 1970, and the Calley court-martial and verdict of 29 March 1971. The country's attention in the spring of 1971 still goes to the war on trial at home: the veterans' protests of April and the Pentagon Papers of June. The Calley argument, in which most Americans sided with the lieutenant, is absent. That argument divided the country along lines that did not run between the war's supporters and its opponents. The 70% who thought Calley a scapegoat¹ included many who also wanted out. Its absence removes a bitterness more than it removes opposition.

### Perverse effect

**F011-11** **No massacre means no reckoning, and the command climate that produced it runs on unexamined.** My Lai's exposure forced the Army to look at how a company could kill hundreds of civilians in a morning and how a division could hide it for a year and a half. The Peers inquiry's findings reached the training of every officer and NCO after 1970, in the law of war, the duty to refuse an unlawful order and the duty to report. Under the removal, the massacre does not happen, but the conditions that produced it do. The body count stays the measure of success, the free-fire areas of Quang Ngai stay in use, and the companies of 1968–71 are the same companies. The educated lieutenants and schooled sergeants of memoranda 007 and 010 act on that climate from below, and they help. They did not prevent My Lai in the record, and they cannot substitute for a reckoning from above that never comes. I judge the cost moderate. It falls on villagers the record does not name, killed in incidents smaller than Son My that went unreported because no one had been made to look.

### Anticipation

**F011-12** **The enforcement threat works only while it is not tested, and its gain is the offensive not begun.** A threat to return in strength is worth most if Hanoi believes it and never tests it, and then its effect is invisible. Under the removal of Watergate the North in 1973–74 faces a President whose word it cannot dismiss as the word of a man about to fall. Its "strategic raids" of 1974 were limited operations meant to grind down the ARVN without provoking a response, and history's Politburo grew bolder as the American political system turned on itself. Here I judge that the North is somewhat more cautious in 1974, with fewer or later raids, and that the caution shows in no metric. It would appear as battles not fought in the Iron Triangle or around Thuong Duc. The anticipation ends where Case–Church begins. Once the North tests the threat at Phuoc Long and nothing comes, the caution is spent in a month, and the invisible gain of 1974 becomes the visible loss of 1975.

### Enemy detection and learning

**F011-13** **Hanoi learns the right lesson about Nixon and the right lesson about Congress, and the second outweighs the first.** The North's analysts read American politics closely and largely correctly. Without Watergate they see a President re-elected by a historic margin, in full control of his administration, who bombed Hanoi in December 1972 and promised Thieu he would do it again¹. They also see the Case–Church amendment, passed by veto-proof margins in June 1973¹, and a Congress that cut the Republic's aid in 1974 even without the scandal. Their conclusion, which I think is right, is that Nixon would respond if he could and that Congress will not let him. That conclusion propagates from the Politburo to the B-2 Front and the regional fronts as caution in 1974 and as the decision to test once at Phuoc Long. The doctrine they derive is that the test must be of a kind that puts the question to Congress rather than to the President: a province, not a city; a border, not a capital. That is the doctrine they applied historically. What they get right that hurts the Republic is that a strong President bound by law is weaker than a strong President, and almost as weak as a weak one.

### Enemy adaptation cost

**F011-14** **The North's counter is cheap: wait, and test with a province.** The adaptation the removal of Watergate demands of Hanoi is patience in 1974 and one test at the start of 1975. The test at Phuoc Long cost the PAVN about 1,300 killed and wounded on the record. If it is held a few months later or made more cautiously, the cost is time in the dry season, which the North had less of than it thought, and which the critical path of 1975 shows was the one thing that could have mattered. The exchange rate is still in the North's favour. The provision costs the United States nothing to keep and the North a season at most to test. It costs the North more than history's adaptation did, because history's North had the scandal to tell it the answer without a test.

### Cross-boundary feedback

**F011-15** **The loop through the draft is short and real: fewer notices, a quieter campus, an easier draft law.** Theater effect: none. The volunteers fight as the draftees fought, and the casualties are the same. Domestic path: a quarter fewer induction notices from 1965, about 464,000 over the war, and perhaps 10–20% fewer men accused of draft offences¹. The draft's politics bite in three fights: the extension of the draft law in 1967, the lottery of December 1969 and the extension of 1971. Each is less bitter. Return path into the theater: the troop requests of 1965–67 meet a draft that costs the administration less, which might have let Johnson draw more men and take less heat for it. Under no troop ceiling, that return path is closed, because the force was already the size the war required. The loop ends at the public's tolerance, which rises at the margin: the public facet moves by perhaps one to three steps over 1966–70². Weighed against POSCP01-1, that gain is real and does not reach the decision of 31 March 1968, which was made by Tet and the casualties.

**F011-16** **The loop through the credibility gap is narrow and slightly positive: MACV's word is worth more in 1970–71.** Theater effect: no cover-up comes apart in November 1969, and the starkest proof that a division's reports could hide a massacre never reaches the press. Domestic path: the credibility gap between what MACV reported and what the press reported is narrower in 1970–71 than history's. Memoranda 002 and 006 had already narrowed it with honest province reports and audited Phoenix counts⁵. Return path: Vietnamization's reports of progress in 1970–72 are credited a little more by Congress and the press, which helps the administration's case for its appropriations in 1971–72. The loop closes small. The war's unpopularity was not mainly a matter of disbelief in MACV's figures by 1970.

**F011-17** **The loop through the 1974 vote returns into the theater as fuel, aircraft and rounds, and into Hanoi as a harder calculation.** Theater effect: the Republic's war of 1974 is fought on about the fiscal 1974 level of supply instead of a third less. Path: fewer aircraft grounded, more rounds at the gun line, fewer posts silent, and perhaps a lower casualty rate in the attrition of 1974, which history put at about 30,000 South Vietnamese dead for the year. Return path to Washington: a Republic visibly fighting and holding through 1974 is a better argument for the fiscal 1976 appropriation and for the April 1975 supplemental. Return path to Hanoi: the North's General Staff, which already counted the Bien Hoa arsenal as part of the Republic's strength after Phuoc Long³, must count a larger American appropriation as well. I follow it until it reaches the critical path of 1975, which is Part 3's test. My expectation is that it reaches the strategic raids of 1974 and the defence of fixed positions, and not the decisions of 13 and 14 March 1975.

### Open channel

**F011-18** **The package changes who Americans think fought the war.** With a quarter of the draftees now volunteers and no My Lai, the soldier of the American memory is somewhat less the unwilling conscript and somewhat less the perpetrator. That affects the reception of the men who came home in 1969–73 and the veterans' movement of 1971. In April 1971 veterans opposed to the war threw their medals onto the Capitol steps. Their protest had the war as its subject, not the draft or My Lai. Its weight came from who the protesters were, and that does not change. I record the channel as real and small, a change in tone rather than in votes.

**F011-19** **The willing soldier compounds with the schooled sergeant and the rotating battalion in the bad years.** In 1969–71 the American rifle company's decay came, on the record of this war, from "the war's politics and the draft rather than from the sergeant". The volunteers act on the draft half of that sentence. In the Americal Division at LZ Baldy, the 23rd Division's inspector general counted about a third fewer drug cases and refusals in platoons whose sergeants held the course and had led them for six months¹². In a platoon where three or four of the draftees are now volunteers, I judge a further tenth to a fifth fewer². This gain is modest. It falls in the years when the Army's indiscipline was most dangerous to its own men, and it does not reach the larger cause, a war the soldiers knew was being given up.

## §2 Part C — Counter-directional finding and strongest positive effect

**F011-20 · Counter-directional** **A promise that stays credible longer makes the Republic plan for a rescue the law forbids.** The removal of Watergate keeps the President who wrote the letters in office, and Thieu kept the letters. Through 1974 nothing tells him the man who wrote them cannot keep them. That is the provision's purpose, and in March 1975 it may be its harm. A Thieu who believes the bombers may return has less reason to shorten his line in the dry season of 1974–75. He has less reason to rebuild a general reserve out of I Corps or to adopt the Dalat study's plan for a phased withdrawal from the Highlands with the families moved first⁶. History's Thieu made the worst decision of the war on 14 March, three days instead of three months and Route 7 instead of a secured road, in part because he had learned from Phuoc Long that no one was coming. Under this provision he learns it later, or less certainly. He may hold longer in the Highlands and then lose them in a worse rout, or delay the decision past the point where any withdrawal could be staffed. I state it at its weight: it is a real cost, of the same order as the provision's gain, and it turns on the same decisions of March 1975 that no provision has yet reached. The rise in what the Republic receives and the rise in what it expects work against each other. Which dominates is Part 3's question.

**F011-21 · Strongest positive** **The Republic fights 1974 on the 1973 allowance, and the question of April 1975 is put by a President who can put it.** The single most significant change is the money and the man together. With the removal of Watergate, the fiscal 1975 appropriation rises to about $850 million – $1.1 billion. Spent through the Republic's own arsenal and battery works, it buys about what $1.0 – 1.4 billion would have bought historically, upper bound marked, roughly holding the Republic at the fiscal 1974 level instead of cutting it by a third. That reaches what memorandum 008 could not: fuel and aircraft. The VNAF flies in the second half of 1974. The relief columns have fuel, and the 155mm and 175mm guns that Bien Hoa could not supply are not rationed as hard. And in April 1975 the request for emergency aid is made by a President with his authority and a Congress with 15–30 more Republicans than history's. I judge that the attrition of 1974 costs the Republic perhaps 1,500–4,000 fewer dead than the historical figure of about 30,000, a judgment resting on a rate of 5–13% fewer losses in fights the army accepted with fuller supply². That sits on top of the 1,000–2,500 that memorandum 008's domestic rounds saved³. The improvement is major for 1974. Whether it moves the date or the outcome of 1975 depends on whether it reaches the critical path, and I do not expect it to.

## §3 Changes to existing forces

### The advisory effort and MACV staff

Changed slightly in 1970–71 and after 1973. In 1970–71 MACV's reports are credited a little more in Washington and the press, because the starkest proof that its chain could hide a massacre never surfaces. MACV's Inspector General studies a command-climate inquiry in 1970 and is not allowed to make it. After March 1973 the Defense Attaché Office under Murray reports to a President who reads its warnings himself. Its fight over the 1974 ration is easier, and its successors' requests in 1975 meet the same Congress.

### US conventional forces

Changed at the margin in 1966–71. About 162,000 of the men who served in Vietnam as draftees serve as volunteers, most in the Army's rifle companies¹. The decay of 1969–71 is a little less in the units where they serve, most of all where a schooled sergeant and a rotating battalion already held it back. The Americal Division's 11th Brigade does not commit the massacre at Son My, and its later record in Quang Ngai stands as history's. No unit's tactics change.

### US Special Forces and CIDG

Unchanged. The Special Forces were volunteers already, and the camps' war turns on nothing the package reaches.

### ARVN divisions and the JGS

Changed in 1974–75 through the appropriation. The divisions fight the attrition of 1974 on about the fiscal 1974 allowance rather than a third less. Their 105mm batteries already drew a third more rounds from Bien Hoa³, and now the 155mm and 175mm batteries and the trucks are less starved. The Joint General Staff plans for 1975 under a President who has not resigned. If that makes it plan for a rescue the law forbids, the plan is worse.

### RF/PF and territorial security

Changed in 1974. The territorial posts' radios stayed on the air under memorandum 008³. Here the relief columns and the helicopters that the posts' defence relied on have fuel more often. In 1968–71 Son Tinh District's war is unchanged in kind.

### Rolling Thunder

Unchanged. Rolling Thunder ended in 1968, before any provision in the package took effect.

### Trail interdiction

Unchanged. The bombing of the Trail ended with Case–Church on 15 August 1973¹, and I judge that it ends on the same date in this war in most cases.

### Arc Light

Unchanged in action, changed in posture. From August 1973 the B-52Js sit on alert at Andersen and U-Tapao as the instrument of the President's promise. They do not fly over Indochina again unless Congress authorizes it, which I judge unlikely, and they would fly without their coordinate cell.

### Airmobile formations

Unchanged. The airmobile divisions' volunteers are counted under the US conventional forces.

### Riverine and coastal forces

Unchanged. The Navy's enlisted men were already volunteers¹, and the package does not reach the riverine war.

### Phoenix / Phung Hoang

Unchanged in action. The audited count that narrowed the credibility gap from 1965 and the removal of My Lai narrow it together, but the programme's work does not change.

### Third-country forces

Unchanged. The Korean, Australian and Thai contingents act on nothing the package touches. The Korean Marine Brigade's war in Quang Ngai and the Korean divisions' record with the population stand as history's.

- **P007a** (US officer education): it acts on the command climate below the company without the Peers inquiry above it.
- **P006a, P002b** (Phoenix and the province Inspectorate): the credibility gap they narrowed narrows further.
