# R04 — The territorials' squad gun: STILL WATER and Bau Bang corrected (erratum E04)

*A revision made for an erratum. It corrects two events' account of what the Regional Forces carried. No event is created or cancelled, no event's outcome changes, and the end state is unchanged.*

## Why

The small arms study ST02 found that two events gave Regional Force companies the M249 squad gun, which the record says they never received (Q-1 · ERRATUM). Memorandum 004 issued the M4A1 carbine to every rifleman of the RVNAF, the territorials included, but held the M249 to the regular US and ARVN squads. Its negative specification for P004c is explicit: "The Regional and Popular Forces do not receive it: the outpost keeps its BAR". Its abandoned employment concept EC004-10 records what happened when some province chiefs diverted squad guns to their RF posts anyway: linked ammunition and spare barrels never reached the posts, the guns burned out or ran dry, and they were recalled to the squads¹. The chapters keep the rule elsewhere. At Cai Lay in 1968 the 311th RF Company had "one squad gun lent by the 7th Division", and no later memorandum issued the gun to the territorials.

Two events written after memorandum 004 broke the rule. EV006-2, written by memorandum 006, has the RF companies of Hoc Mon and Go Vap holding STILL WATER's cordons "with M4A1 carbines and their M249s". EV007-3, in the Bau Bang paragraph memorandum 009 added, has the rear RF company putting "its two M249s into the treeline". The erratum is E04.

## What changes, event by event

**EV006-2 · Operation STILL WATER · 8 Mar – 30 Jun 1965.** The Hoc Mon and Go Vap RF companies now hold the cordons with M4A1 carbines and the BARs that the territorials kept as their automatic rifles. Nothing else in the event changes. STILL WATER was a police operation: the Police Field Force and the Gia Dinh PRU made the arrests, the Special Branch's lists chose the men, and the RF companies held the ring at night. What broke the Hoc Mon committee was the lists and the magistrates, not the weight of fire on the cordon, and the cordons were never seriously tested.

**EV007-3 · Operation OPEN ROAD · 3–21 May 1966, the Bau Bang convoy.** The rear company now puts its BARs into the treeline in the first two minutes, and the event says that the Regional Forces never drew the squad gun. The judgment on the convoy stands. Memorandum 009 credited the lower losses, about 32 RF dead rather than about 40, to the six-week territorial course: the men left the trucks on the side away from the fire, fought from the ditch, returned fire in pairs and reported the enemy on the sector net²³. None of that depended on a belt-fed gun. The Browning, with its 20-round magazine, gave the rear company a shorter burst of covering fire than an M249 would have, and the margin it bought is thinner than the old text implied; it is not thin enough to move a judgment that already carried a wide range. The 272nd Regiment's conclusion, that the territorials "now fight from the ditch" and its ambushes must kill in the first volley or not be laid, reads more truly for it: the regiment was answering the soldiers' training, not their weapon.

## Effect on the war

None on the course of the war. No end vector moves, no settled position is reversed, and the end state in `history/endstate.toml` is unchanged. The correction restores a line memorandum 004 drew deliberately. The territorials carried the best rifle in the country and the oldest automatic rifle, and the outpost wall at night was held with the carbine and the BAR. That is the gap the squad gun's abandoned concept exposed, and it stays open in the record as it was meant to.

Erratum E04 and revision R04 answer this study's Q-1. The Regional Forces never drew the M249: P004c's negative specification keeps the BAR at the outpost, EC004-10 recalled the squad guns the province chiefs diverted, and EV000-42's lent gun at Cai Lay is the only squad gun in RF hands in the chapters¹. EV006-2 now has the Hoc Mon and Go Vap RF companies holding STILL WATER's cordons with M4A1 carbines and BARs, and EV007-3 has the rear RF company at Bau Bang putting its BARs, not two M249s, into the treeline. The study's Phase II passage on Bau Bang should read accordingly. Its point holds and is sharper for the correction: the RF losses fell from about 40 to about 32 because of the territorial course, not the weapon², and the 272nd Regiment was answering the territorials' training. In this war the territorials fought the whole of 1964–69 with the carbine and the Browning, the outpost's automatic weapon never changed, and the squad gun's margin belongs to the regular infantry alone. No study-held value changes.
