# CP01 — Part 2: Trajectory and limits

The war as the eleven provisions have made it goes where the historical war went, by a better road through the countryside and the same road everywhere else. This part says, phase by phase, where it goes from here with the force now held; names the constraint that binds each line of effort; reviews every gap; prices what the force still lacks and turns the bill into a menu; and settles the question of the phases.

## 8. Revised trajectory

**Phase I (January 1964 – July 1965).** The Republic gets the instruments of a government on its first day and keeps them through five changes of government because the American lever makes repeal dearer than survival. The held belt begins: titles, posts, chiefs who stay, platoons that are not outgunned. Hanoi commits its regulars on its own schedule, and the United States commits its army on its own. Nothing about the phase's outcome is in doubt and nothing in the force moves it; what it moves is the starting line of every later phase in the Delta and the coastal Centre.

**Phase II (August 1965 – December 1967).** The trajectory runs on two levels. Above, the main-force war of attrition goes exactly as it went, because the enemy still chooses his battles and regenerates in Cambodia and Laos. Below, the held belt is cleared of resident infrastructure before Tet rather than after it, and the measured siege at Con Thien gives Westmoreland a lesson he applies in the wrong place. The force held at this checkpoint cannot do better here, because the only thing that would change this phase's outcome is an instrument that finds and fixes a regiment under the canopy, and no provision supplies one.

**Phase III (1968).** Tet's political verdict is fixed. Its military course is better in the Delta and at Khe Sanh and worse in Hue. The trajectory from here depends on one thing the force does not supply: what the Republic does in the countryside in the eighteen months after the Front's local forces die. It starts that work from a register and a Special Branch four years old, which is the best start any version of this war has had.

**Phase IV (1969–71).** The best years in the countryside and the worst on the frontier, as historically, with the countryside further on. The trajectory turns here, and not in 1975. The American lever lapses with the withdrawal, the institutions of 1964 are captured by the palace without being abolished, and the Republic's conventional army is taught by Lam Son 719 to rely on bombs it will not keep. If any force could be added that changes the end state, it must act in this phase: either by making the American withdrawal leave behind something the Republic can own, or by keeping something American in the country after 1973.

**Phase V (1972).** The Easter Offensive is stopped by American air power and American advisers, more cheaply and more decisively at the sieges than in history. Its lesson is the historical one, sharpened: the Republic holds where the Americans fight for it. The agreement of January 1973 removes the Americans and leaves the PAVN in the South.

**Phase VI (1973–75).** Nothing in the force as held survives the American withdrawal except what was Vietnamese from the start: the land settlement, the chiefs' experience, the files, the carbines and, for a year, the ammunition. Of these only the last two fight, and the ration of 1974 takes the ammunition. The PAVN's offensive of 1975 is a conventional campaign by corps with armour and artillery, against an army spread across the whole country without reserves, without air, and without the American promise. It falls on its date.

### The end state

The end state stays at rung (a), collapse: the Republic falls on 30 April 1975, on the historical timeline (`history/endstate.toml`, unchanged).

The next rung up, (b), dependent survival, is not reached because the force has changed nothing that decides whether the Republic survives without American combat forces, and nothing that keeps those forces in the country. The record is plain about why. The provisions have worked where the war was political and local, and in the air where the enemy holds still (G-12 at the siege). The war of 1972–75 was decided by three things none of them touched: a PAVN main force regenerated in sanctuary, an American clock that the casualties of 1965–68 and Tet had already spent, and a Republic whose conventional forces could not defend a besieged town without American bombers or fight a week of contact without American ammunition. POS001-4 said after the first memorandum that political gains are necessary and not sufficient; four more memoranda have confirmed it and added a corollary: military gains that depend on the American presence are not sufficient either, and some of them make the collapse more certain when the presence ends.

The distance to (b) is not the distance to (c) or (d), and I want to say what it is. Rung (b) needs one of two things: an American combat presence that outlasts 1973, which is a political change the exercise models and no provision has touched; or a Republic that survives the 1975 offensive for longer than the historical one did, which needs an army that can hold a corps area for a season without American air. The force as held is three provisions away from the second at the least: one that lets the RVNAF find and fix a PAVN corps without American aircraft, one that gives it air support it owns, and one that gives it the ammunition and fuel to fight without the American appropriation. That is the honest measure of the gap.

## 9. Limiting factors

Ranked by how directly each blocks the next rung:

1. **The RVNAF's dependence on American air, coordinates and ammunition for the conventional war**. It decides 1974–75 and nothing in the force contests it. It binds the line of effort that matters most for rung (b): the defence of the Republic after the Americans leave.
2. **The enemy's control of engagement and his sanctuaries**. It binds the main-force war in every phase. The aircraft have made him easier to see and the bombs have made the siege expensive for him, and he still chooses every other fight and regenerates beyond reach. While this holds, attrition has no end and the Americans cannot stay long enough for it to matter.
3. **The American clock**. It binds everything after 1968. No provision has moved it a band, and the one provision that touched the cause of its spending, the radar, sharpened Tet rather than softening it.
4. **The pool of honest, able officers**. It binds the RVNAF's leadership, the provinces' government and every institution of 1964 at once. It is why the divisions stay weak while the provinces improve.
5. **The American lever and its lapse**. It binds the survival of every Vietnamese institution the provisions created; when it lapses in 1970–71 they are captured. It binds rung (b) only indirectly, through the regime's narrowing.
6. **Trail throughput above requirement**. It binds the North's capacity to sustain the war in the South; the radar measures it and nothing cuts it below requirement. It is ranked here because the requirement in 1975 is a conventional army's and the Trail meets it.
7. **Night security outside the held belt**. It binds pacification in the contested belt and is the line on which land, police and rifles all stop. It is ranked low only because the countryside is not where the Republic is lost in 1975.

## 10. Gap register review

- **G-1** · A state without a foundation · narrowed · Land, police, merit chiefs and the Commission give the Republic a government in the held belt from 1964; it is captured by the palace when the American lever lapses in 1970–71, and its foundation in the countryside does not carry into the conventional war (CP01)

- **G-13** · No single manager of the war · open · CORDS on its historical date and form; the provisions created no single manager, only better inheritances for it (CP01)
- **G-14** · No lever on Hanoi's will · open · Nothing in the force reaches the Politburo; reform raised its urgency without changing its decisions (CP01)

What moved the narrowed gaps is plain: G-1, G-2 and G-3 by memoranda 001, 002 and 004 together; G-10 and G-11 by memorandum 003; G-12 by memorandum 005. What would close them is plainer still, and the same for all three of the countryside gaps: night security beyond the held belt, which is men present after dark with a radio and a reaction behind them, in the contested hamlets where none of the existing programmes reach. The open gaps that bind the end state, G-4, G-5, G-7 and G-8, are open for the reasons in section 9, and G-6 and G-16 are open in a way that a single institutional provision could reach: how the Americans who advise and fight are chosen, kept and rotated.

## 11. The bill

What the force still lacks, ranked by what it would do for the next rung:

1. **Vietnamese-owned air support that can use what American reconnaissance finds.** The siege box was an American instrument from the radar to the bomb, and when it left the Republic had nothing. A light attack force the VNAF can own, fly and maintain, with guided weapons that do not need a 1760 bus and a Vietnamese targeting cell, would contest the dependence that decides 1974–75.
2. **Night vision and radios for the men who hold the hamlets.** Every programme stops at the edge of what the territorials can hold after dark. A Popular Forces platoon that can see at night and call its reaction force by radio is the one instrument that moves that edge.
3. **A means of finding the main force under the canopy.** The enemy's control of engagement is the war's central fact. Ground sensors that report movement under the trees, laid where the radar's tracks vanish, are the nearest real instrument, and they act on G-7 and G-9 at once.
4. **The officers.** No equipment answers G-4. An institution that selects, trains and keeps the RVNAF's junior leaders, and an American advisory system that sends its best men for long enough to know their counterparts, are the two halves of the same answer.
5. **Helicopter hours for the RVNAF.** The reaction that holds a post at night, and the mobility that lets a division fight a corps, both ride on lift the Republic does not own.
6. **Night firepower over the contested hamlets.** A gunship that sees at night and fires precisely is the reaction that arrives in the dark when the road is cut.
7. **Ammunition and fuel the Republic can sustain.** Not a provision of equipment: a stock and a production base outside the annual American appropriation. I name it because it decides 1974, and I do not put it on the menu because no 2026 system supplies it.

## 12. Phase redraw

No memorandum has proposed a redraw (001–005 §4.N each declined), and the war does not warrant one. Every phase boundary still marks a turn that happened on its date: the July decision, Tet, Nixon's inauguration, the Easter Offensive, the Paris Agreement. The provisions have changed the interior of every phase and the boundary of none. `history/phases.toml` is unchanged.

## 13. Method

Two corrections to method follow from Part 1. The first answers the finding that every chain in five memoranda resolved exactly as predicted: a war that never surprises its analysts is being written too tidily. The second answers the calibration of the rifles' lives-saved figure, a war-wide number resting mostly on one asserted rate.

- **DIRCP01-1** · In each Part 3, take at least one causal chain from an earlier memorandum whose later links fall in a phase this memorandum revises, and test it against the war as now written; where the new provision or free play makes it resolve differently, say so in the chain review and let it (every chain in memoranda 001–005 resolved as predicted, which is more likely tidiness than foresight)
- **DIRCP01-2** · A war-wide figure for lives saved, or any war-wide count built from a rate, must be shown at work in at least two dated events in the chapters before a later memorandum repeats it; until then it is cited as a judgment resting on its rate, with the rate named (CP01 calibration of the¹ lives-saved figure)

The settled positions from this checkpoint are in Part 1 (POSCP01-1 to POSCP01-3).

Checkpoint CP01, after five memoranda, found the war better in its interior and identical at its end. The provisions of 1964 gave the Republic a government in its held belt four to six years early, made the enemy's rear a measured fact, armed the territorials as well as their attackers, and made every measured siege the enemy's worst battle. They touched neither his control of the battle elsewhere, nor his sanctuaries, nor the American clock, and three of them deepened the Republic's dependence on American air, coordinates and ammunition, which decided 1974–75. Phases I and II were reviewed and consolidated; every chain resolved as predicted, which the checkpoint treats as a warning. The end state stays at rung (a). The bill is led by air support the Republic can own, night vision and radios for the territorials, and a way to find the main force under canopy.
