# Trajectory Log

One entry per memorandum: how its provision shifted the course of the war, and where the end state moved. Entries are appended by `scripts/fie.py` from the memorandum's log block and never edited. Read top to bottom, this is the arc of the war across all provisions.

## Memo 001 — Accelerated land-to-the-tiller reform and an enforced GVN anti-corruption programme

Memorandum 001 gave the Republic land reform and an anti-corruption commission on the war's first day. The war's interior moved; its outline did not. About 1,150,000 hectares were titled by 1971, six years ahead of history, and where the government held the night its arrival became a guarantee rather than the landlord's return: the Delta's Front recruited less, filled its battalions with northerners sooner and in 1969 conceded the land issue. The Commission counted the army honestly, convicted a general and audited Phoenix, then lost its American lever with the drawdown and was curbed in 1971. Every main-force battle, Tet's political verdict, the Easter Offensive and the fall of Saigon on 30 April 1975 stand. The end state stays at rung (a). The provisions first touched G-1, the political deficit, and showed that moving it is necessary but not sufficient: a South with more to hold still needs the means to hold it against a conventional invasion after the Americans leave.

## Memo 002 — National Police reform with a rural constabulary, and merit-based selection of province and district chiefs

Memorandum 002 gave the Republic a rural police, a Special Branch and province and district chiefs chosen by examination, on the same day as the land reform. Together with 001 they made a government in the district four to five years early: chiefs from the list held their districts through the coups, constables lived beside the Popular Forces, and the infrastructure in the government's own belt was driven out before Tet, as Operation NIGHT LANTERN showed in Can Giuoc in 1966. The costs came as predicted: constables killed in posts settled ahead of security, a war on the best chiefs, Ky's capture of the police against the Buddhists, and in Hue the Republic's own census turned into execution lists. Thieu captured the Board as the American lever lapsed, and the historical police closed the gap after 1969. The main-force war, Tet's verdict in Washington, the Easter Offensive and the fall of Saigon on 30 April 1975 stand. The end state stays at rung (a): the political deficit is now largely met where the government holds the night, and the conventional one is untouched.

## Memo 003 — Two U-2S Dragon Lady and four RQ-4B Global Hawk Block 40 high-altitude reconnaissance aircraft

Memorandum 003 gave this command two U-2S and four Global Hawks on the war's first day, and with them the first honest picture of the enemy's rear. The war's record moved; its outcome did not. The coastal radar closed the trawler route in 1964 and pushed the enemy's southern supply into Sihanoukville eighteen months early. On 4 August 1964 the Gulf watch saw no torpedo boats, Pierce Arrow was never flown, and the war's only legislative authority rested on the real attack of 2 August. From 1965 the Trail, the North's roads and the Cambodian route were counted rather than guessed, so the bombing's failures and the inflated truck claims reached Washington in weeks rather than years. The radar mapped the 1970 incursion's caches and struck the 1972 armour through cloud, but it deepened the fixation on Khe Sanh before Tet, saw nothing under the canopy or in the hamlets, and left with the Americans. Saigon still falls on 30 April 1975, at rung (a).

## Memo 004 — Modern infantry small arms for US, ARVN and RF/PF: M4A1 URG-I with Aimpoint CompM5, M110A2 squad marksman rifle, M249 squad automatic weapon

Memorandum 004 gave every American and South Vietnamese soldier and every territorial a modern carbine from 1964, with a belt-fed gun and a marksman in each regular squad. Its effect was concentrated where the war was decided first: at the outpost wall after dark. In the held belt a post with its platoon now held nine attacks in ten, and the land and police programmes had garrisons that won the first thirty seconds. The general reserve came through 1965 battered but whole, the M16 crisis of 1967 never happened, and at Tet a third of the attacks on the district towns failed at the walls. Perhaps 10,000–25,000 South Vietnamese and 1,000–2,500 American lives were saved, mostly before 1969, when the historical army caught up. Against this, the Front armed itself with our carbines, and the army that met the 1974 ration was more dependent on American ammunition and lost its hoards with I Corps. The enemy still chose every battle. Saigon falls on 30 April 1975, at rung (a).

## Memo 005 — B-52J Stratofortress replacing every B-52, with GBU-31 and GBU-38 JDAM and Quickstrike mines

Memorandum 005 replaced every B-52 with the B-52J and gave it GPS bombs and modern mines, and found that precision changes what dies, not who wins. A coordinate cell built from the U-2S's imagery made every fixed target a one-night kill, and Washington refused to use it in the North for seven years. In the panhandle the bombers cut every measured crossing in 1966 and taught Group 559 to build a net with no single point of failure, so the Trail delivered as before. In the sieges the effect was transforming: Con Thien, Khe Sanh, An Loc and Kontum became the enemy's worst battles, sparing perhaps 1,400–2,700 allied lives, and after Khe Sanh the PAVN stopped laying sieges until 1972 forced it to. Linebacker II lost six bombers, not fifteen, and Bach Mai was not hit. But the ARVN learned to hold besieged towns with American bombers it could never own, and in 1974 it waited at Thuong Duc and Phuoc Long for bombers that did not come. Saigon falls on 30 April 1975, at rung (a).

## CP01 — Checkpoint

Checkpoint CP01, after five memoranda, found the war better in its interior and identical at its end. The provisions of 1964 gave the Republic a government in its held belt four to six years early, made the enemy's rear a measured fact, armed the territorials as well as their attackers, and made every measured siege the enemy's worst battle. They touched neither his control of the battle elsewhere, nor his sanctuaries, nor the American clock, and three of them deepened the Republic's dependence on American air, coordinates and ammunition, which decided 1974–75. Phases I and II were reviewed and consolidated; every chain resolved as predicted, which the checkpoint treats as a warning. The end state stays at rung (a). The bill is led by air support the Republic can own, night vision and radios for the territorials, and a way to find the main force under canopy.

## Memo 006 — Phung Hoang (Phoenix) formalized and modernized from 1964; US unit rotation in place of individual replacement; a selected advisory corps on 18-month tours with 2026 training

Memorandum 006 gave the Republic Phung Hoang in 1964, built behind the police posts with warrants, an audit and no quotas; it gave the American battalions unit rotation and the ARVN a selected advisory corps on eighteen-month tours. The first was the largest change: the Front's district committees in the held belt were followed out of the villages and broken in 1965–66, at Hoc Mon and Can Giuoc, three years before the historical Phoenix reached that layer, and the count was honest throughout. But success pushed the method into the contested belt, a raid at Mo Duc made "Phoenix" a scandal in 1967, and the national file, consolidated by the palace in 1971, was taken intact when Saigon fell. Unit rotation made the American battalion fight better in the contacts it was granted and gave the enemy a relief week to aim at. The advisers deepened influence and left. The end state stays at rung (a): Saigon falls on 30 April 1975.

## Memo 007 — Officer education to the 2026 standard for new US and ARVN officers from 1957, gating promotion; ARVN officer selection and promotion reform

Memorandum 007 educated every American and ARVN officer commissioned from 1957 to the 2026 standard and gave the ARVN a promotion board that chose its battalion commanders on merit, kept them eighteen months and relieved them when they failed. From 1966 the ARVN's battalions were led as the historical army's never were, and the enemy answered as he answers every better unit: he declined to fight them. The 272nd Regiment withdrew from its ambush on Route 13 rather than meet a battalion that walked its flanks, and attacked a Regional Force convoy instead; about a third fewer battalions were broken in 1966–67, and no metric showed it. But the reform stopped where the American lever stopped. The list for generals became advisory in 1970, the palace purged the colonels who asked for it back, and Quang Tri in 1972 and the Highlands in 1975 were lost by generals no board had chosen. The army's best staff plan became Thieu's warrant for the retreat of March 1975. Saigon falls on 30 April 1975, at rung (a).

## Memo 008 — A South Vietnamese industrial base one generation above 1964, standing from 1 January 1964: metallurgy and machining; electronics manufacturing

Memorandum 008 gave the Republic, from 1964, the steelworks, arsenal, radio plant and battery works of a country a generation ahead of it, and made its army buy from them from 1965. For ten years the effect was slow and mostly invisible: radios at every post of the held belt, attacked a third less often and answered, after the Front began ambushing the relief at Cai Lay in 1967, by guns instead of trucks; bridges back in weeks; and a rebuild loop that absorbed Vietnamization's equipment. In 1974 it was the first provision to reach the Republic's sustainment after the Americans left: the last aid dollar bought about a third more, the squad guns were fed and the radios stayed on the air. It changed what the vote bought and not the vote, and it reached none of the decisions of March 1975. Tested against the critical path, it delayed the collapse by days, not the three weeks that would have mattered. The 4th Corps took the plants intact. Saigon falls on 30 April 1975, at rung (a).
